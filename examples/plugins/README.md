# plugins (the effect gallery)

One live HTML form inside `<canvas content="drawable">`, and every effect plugin of this repository applied to it. The canvas is the first thing on the page, on a phone as on a desktop: a bar with the title and a frame readout, a strip of chips (one per effect, plus "Effect off"), then the stage. The form stays live under every effect: click a field and type.

- **Picking an effect** applies it to the form with its own controls (rendered as HTML inside the form by the plugin's `mountUI`). A plugin with an action `demo` plays it once, a short showcase of what it does (not when the user prefers reduced motion; any input takes over). `?effect=<name>` selects an effect on load (`?effect=off` none), `&demo=0` without the showcase, and `&opt.<plugin>.<option>=<value>` sets an option of that plugin over its defaults (for example `&opt.water.intensity=1`; numbers and `true` / `false` are read as such).
- **A second effect over the first**: the smaller strip of chips under the effects ("+ on top") adds one more plugin to the same host. The host runs its plugins in the order they were added: the first reads the drawn form, the second reads the picture the first rendered, and a click is mapped back through both to the field you see. The first effect also reads the pointer through the second (`context.localPoint`), so a drop of water under a curved CRT screen, or under a tilted card, lands under the finger. With two effects the form has a small switch between their controls, and the panel describes both. `&then=<name>` selects the second effect on load (with `?effect=<name>`; `&opt.<plugin>.<option>=<value>` reaches either). Every ordered pair of the gallery's plugins runs; what a pair looks like is the plugins' business (a transition plugin on top, such as Shatter or Disintegrate, takes the first effect's picture apart, which is the point).
- **Input map** (the button in the bar, `&map=1`) shows what the host does with the pointer. An effect moves pixels, so the field you see under the pointer is elsewhere in the page; the host maps the pointer back through every plugin (`host.mapPoint`) and acts on the element at that place. Over the stage the map draws the pointer (a white ring), the place in the page it is sent to (a pink dot, joined to the ring by a dashed line), the frame of the real element there, in its true position under the canvas, and a label with the element, what it holds now and both points. Under an effect that moves nothing the two points coincide; under the holo card, the CRT monitor or a curled page they do not, and the frame is not where the field is drawn. It is page chrome outside the canvas, drawn again only when the pointer moves or the effect presents a frame.
- **The scene** is what lies beside the form in the same canvas, chosen with the switch drawn at its top left (ordinary HTML in the canvas, pressed through the effect like the form). *Form*, the default, is the page as it always was. *Daylight* is a bright page with a landscape painted in CSS gradients: bright, colourful content gives water, glass and foil something to refract and to reflect, which a dark page does not. *Live data* is a clock, ten bars and a feed that change once a second: content that keeps changing is drawn again each time, so with it the page is not at rest, by design. `?scene=daylight` or `?scene=live` selects one on load, with any `?effect=`. The form's own card keeps its dark look in every scene (the plugins draw their controls light on dark).
- **The panel** beside the stage on wide screens, under it elsewhere, describes the selected plugin from its manifest entry: title, version, description, its actions as buttons (`handle.trigger`, with the state each resolves), what it does under reduced motion (`meta.reducedMotion`), the options of its `meta.defaults` (what the page passes, under any `&opt.` of the link) and a link to its source. "About this page" says what the readout counts, the display rate the page measured and whether `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is in effect.
- **The readout** (top right) counts the `effectframe` events of the last second: effect frames per second, the interval between frames (median / 95th percentile, ms), the frame cap and the polyfill's mode. At rest it reads 0, unless the plugin is continuous by design (`meta.continuous`); it stops counting itself when no frame arrives.
- **The frame cap slider** (`canvas.frameCap`, inside the form) defaults to the display's rate, measured from 90 animation frames while the page starts (the 20th percentile of their intervals, which a busy frame cannot shorten); the page reports ready only after that, so the slider never changes the drawn form later. At its top the canvas is uncapped (`frameCap` 0, exactly the display rate).
- **The line under the title** is a short hint for the selected effect, for a finger on a touch screen and for a mouse elsewhere ("Move over the card to tilt it"); on a touch screen a plugin's `meta.touch` wins ("Drag the glass with a finger; tap through it to use the form."). Nothing on the page claims a touch: a drag on the stage scrolls the page unless it starts on a plugin's own handle, and the form fits the stage without an inner scroll.

Run: `npm run build && npm run demo`, then open `http://127.0.0.1:8765/examples/plugins/` in a browser with WebGPU.

How it works:
- The list comes from `dist/plugins/manifest.json`, which the build writes from every `plugins/<name>/` folder ([plugins/README.md](../../plugins/README.md)). A new plugin appears here without any change to this page.
- The page loads the core and the effects bundle; a plugin bundle (`dist/plugins/<name>.iife.min.js`) is loaded when it is first selected. Switching removes the previous plugin from the host (which also removes its controls) and adds the new one with the `defaults` of its manifest entry.
- Without WebGPU (or when `createHost` rejects) the page says so above the canvas, disables the effects and draws the same form through the plain 2D `drawElementImage` path, decided BEFORE any `getContext` call.

The effects host, the plugins and the frame cap are extensions of this library, not part of the HTML-in-Canvas proposal; browsers' native implementations do not provide them; the page runs them over the polyfill and over a browser's own implementation alike ([docs/guides/EFFECTS-PLUGINS.md](../../docs/guides/EFFECTS-PLUGINS.md#extensions-not-the-standard)).

Checked in headless Chrome:
- `npm run test:examples`: no console or page error, the canvas is not blank, every plugin of the manifest is listed, selecting it mounts its controls, changes the presented pixels and fills the panel (title, version, description, reduced-motion behaviour), "Effect off" removes them, typing reaches the live field, and the slider changes `canvas.frameCap`. A second run hides `navigator.gpu` and checks the message, the disabled effects and typing on the 2D path.
- `npm run test:gallery` (`scripts/check-gallery.mjs`): at 390 x 844 and 1194 x 748 with touch emulation, for every plugin and the template plugin: the stage entirely in the first viewport, the form without an inner scroll, the demo played on selection (and not under reduced motion), 0 effect frames over 2 s once the plugin settled (unless `meta.continuous`), a touch drag on the stage that scrolls the page, and the frame cap at the display rate; at 375 x 667, a short phone, the form without an inner scroll for every plugin and scene; for the daylight and the live scene, with the water plugin: the scene shown and no other, the stage in the first viewport, no inner scroll, a touch tap on the drawn switch that selects another scene through the effect and comes back, and, in the live scene, frames presented while its content changes and none once the form scene is back; for a second effect over the first: picked with a tap and taken away again, the switch between the two effects' controls, no frame at rest, a drop of water that lands under the finger through a curved CRT screen, every ordered pair of plugins, and the input map (it names and frames the field a tap is sent to under a curved screen); each check with a positive control that must turn it red.
- `npm run test:plugins`: the end-to-end test of each plugin drives this page with trusted input: clicks through the effect, its triggers, the frame cap and reduced motion.
