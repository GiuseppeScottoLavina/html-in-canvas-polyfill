# ink

The live page as wet ink on a thin layer of water. A pointer that moves over it stirs an incompressible 2D fluid; the flow carries the page's own pixels along, so the picture smears, swirls and stretches as marbling does, and then the page heals back to its layout. A hovering pointer only stirs the water gently (the page moves by at most 3 CSS px and the moving surface catches the light); a pressed pointer or a finger stirs it hard and releases pigment: two inks side by side that the wake folds into each other and that fade by age. A tap lets a drop of ink fall: it spreads over the water, gathers a darker rim and breaks up into tendrils. Clicks and typing are remapped through the displacement to the element the user sees, mid-swirl. At rest it costs nothing: no effect frame, no animation loop, and the picture is the source, texel for texel.

An **example** effect plugin of this library, not part of the polyfill: nothing in `src/` imports it, and the polyfill, the frame cap and the effects host work without it. The effects host and its plugins are extensions of this library, not part of the HTML-in-Canvas proposal; native implementations do not provide them ([details](../../docs/guides/EFFECTS-PLUGINS.md#extensions-not-the-standard)). Like the host, it is **experimental** ([STABILITY.md](../../docs/STABILITY.md)): its options, actions and look may change in any minor release.

## What it does

1. **The fluid** (`sim.js`, on the CPU). A plane flow in a closed box, the canvas, in vorticity and stream function form, on cells of about 10 CSS px (112 x 64 for a 1100 x 620 canvas), 120 steps per second whatever the display rate. Each step solves the Poisson equation for the stream function with one multigrid V cycle (red-black Gauss-Seidel, five grids, started from the previous solution: the residual falls by about 20 a cycle), takes the velocity as the curl of the cubic B-spline of the stream function, and carries the vorticity along the flow (semi-Lagrangian with a midpoint trace and a MacCormack correction, limited to the values around the departure point). A velocity that is the curl of a smooth stream function is divergence free wherever it is read, whatever the accuracy of the solve: nothing in the flow gathers or thins the page or the pigment.
2. **Vorticity confinement** (Fedkiw, Stam and Jensen 2001) gives back the rotation that a coarse grid smears out. Its force is added as its curl, which is a transport of the vorticity up its own gradient, by upwind fluxes; the result is limited to the largest vorticity before the step, the invariant of a plane flow, so confinement restores swirls and cannot feed them at any strength.
3. **The page** is a displacement field `d` on the same grid: the pixel at `x` shows the source at `x - d(x)`. The flow carries it like the vorticity, and it gains the path of each step (the page moves with the water). It relaxes to zero (the option `heal`), so the page comes home in a second or two.
4. **Rest in finite time.** The vorticity decays (the drag of a thin layer, the option `flow`) and loses 2 /s² on top; the displacement relaxes and loses 4 CSS px/s on top. A decay with a constant loss reaches exactly zero after a finite time, so there is no threshold at which something snaps. The same loss holds the page still where the water moves slower than 4 CSS px/s: the displacement is exactly zero outside a bounded region, and there the output is the source texel.
5. **Stirring.** The pointer pushes the water under it over a radius of 32 CSS px. A push is added as its curl: a pair of counter-rotating swirls either side of the path. As the wake of a body in water sheds swirls to alternate sides, the push sways sideways with a period of five radii along the path, so a stroke leaves a street of swirls and not a straight jet. A pressed pointer or a finger pushes the water towards its own velocity by 0.5 of the difference per pass (times `intensity`; the push is relative to the water, so it saturates). A hovering pointer sets the water under it moving at 0.11 of its own speed, but never faster than 26 CSS px/s (both times `intensity`): however often and however fast it passes, the water it drives stays that slow, and the page under a hover sweep moves by at most 3 CSS px at the defaults (measured: 1.3 px at 400 CSS px/s, 2.8 px at 4000 CSS px/s, against 5 to 91 px before this bound). Three pixels keep 14 to 16 px text readable: a word slides as a whole by less than the gap between two lines, while the swirl reads as water under the pointer; the light on the moving surface (8) makes it visible.
6. **A drop.** A press (or a tap) lets a drop of ink fall where it lands. The drop is thick: its pigment is released five times as dense as a stroke's, one ink around a core of the next, and it spreads over the surface for 0.45 s with a radial flow of its own, u = U(t) (1 - s)² (p - c) / L, s = r² / R², within the reach R of its centre c (L = 60 CSS px, U(t) = U₀ (1 - t / 0.45 s)², U₀ = 1500 CSS px/s times `intensity`). That flow is not divergence free (the drop brings its own liquid): its divergence, 2 U (1 - s)(1 - 3 s) / L, is positive in the middle and negative towards the front, where the flow slows down and converges, so the pigment (and the page, which moves with the water) thins in the middle and piles up into a rim. The reach is uneven, R = L (1 + E(a) + F(a) w(r)) at the angle a around the centre, with amplitudes and phases drawn per drop from a hash of its number (no two drops alike, and no symmetry): `E`, the modes 2 to 4 (0.06 to 0.12 each, none dominant), shapes the flow that also moves the page, on the fluid's grid; `F`, the modes 5 to 24 (up to 0.5 / k^0.8 each), shapes the pigment alone, per texel in `advect.wgsl` (the grid of 10 CSS px could not carry them, and pigment is colour only, so the input mapper is not concerned), weighted by w(r) = smoothstep(0.35 L, 0.85 L, r) so that it frays the front into a ragged edge without streaking the middle. After 0.2 s one to three pushes of uneven strength at random places of the front (pairs of swirls) draw tendrils out of it. The flow of the drop is prescribed and stops by itself; the swirls of the tendrils decay as every other. A pointer that was moving when pressed also throws the drop the way it was heading. Around a recent drop the ink ends in a crisp wet edge (9).
7. **The pigment** (`advect.wgsl`, on the GPU) lives in an rgba16float texture of about one texel per CSS px: the densities of three inks and their age. Every step each texel traces back through the same flow (the curl of the B-spline of the stream function of that very step, a midpoint step) and reads the pigment there with a Catmull-Rom filter limited to the four texels around the point, which keeps filaments a few texels wide over hundreds of steps. Where a drop spreads, the trace adds its radial flow (the formula of `sim.js` with the fine modes of its reach, evaluated per texel) and the pigment is scaled by `exp(-div u dt)`, so that it is carried as a density: it thins where the drop spreads out and gathers where its front converges. A pressed pointer releases two inks side by side along its path (a Gaussian across the path, integrated along each segment with the error function, so consecutive segments join without beads). Ink is capped at a density of 1.2 however often the pen passes; a drop may be released denser, and the pen does not thin it. Successive strokes start at another place of the palette (the golden ratio), and the hue drifts along a stroke.
8. **Fading by age.** Pigment keeps its full strength for 1.3 s and has faded out when it is 2.9 s old, whatever its density: a stroke stays whole while it is drawn and folded, and its tail goes in the order it was drawn. All pigment is therefore gone 2.9 s after the last release, which the plugin knows without reading anything back from the GPU.
9. **The picture** (`ink.wgsl`), per device pixel, in linear light: the displacement as a cubic B-spline of the field (no grid shows); where the map squeezes the page, up to 6 taps of the source along the squeezed direction (the largest singular value of the Jacobian, from the same B-spline), so stretched text does not alias; the pigment as a layer of absorbing and scattering particles over the page (Kubelka and Munk): the absorption `K` and the scattering `S` of the three inks add up by density, and the layer's reflectance `R` and transmittance `T` over a page of reflectance `Rg` give `R + T² Rg / (1 - R Rg)` per channel. Over a bright page the ink glazes it; over a dark page it shows by the light its particles scatter. Within 108 CSS px of a drop that landed less than 3 s ago (outside the middle 10 px), the ink ends in a crisp wet edge: it is cut where its density (before it fades by age, so that the stain fades as a whole) falls under 0.05, with a coverage of one device pixel (the density over its screen-space derivative, `fwidth`), and it is up to 1.8 times as dense in a line about 1.2 CSS px wide inside that edge, the ring of pigment at the edge of a wet stain. Strokes keep their soft edges.
10. **The surface.** Where the water moves and the page with it, its surface catches a soft overhead light. A free surface sits lower where the water is faster (Bernoulli: h = -|u|² / 2g, here h = -0.012 |u|² CSS px for u in CSS px/s); its slope comes from the second derivatives of the same B-spline of the stream function (continuous: no facets), with a soft limit at 0.35, and tilts the normal. The light added is the change of a broad specular lobe (a light 27° from the zenith, up and to the left; a lobe of power 24) from that of the flat surface, times 0.12, in linear light: a faint glint on a dark page, a faint shading on a bright one, a gloss on the ink after a stroke, and exactly 0 where the water is still (the page heals there with no light added). A surface that tilts away from the light darkens the page by at most half, so a dark page shows no black shadow. Half a level of dither where there is pigment or sheen. Where the field is zero and there is no pigment, the source texel goes out untouched.

## Usage

```html
<script src="dist/hic-live-polyfill.iife.min.js"></script>
<script src="dist/hic-live-effects.iife.min.js"></script>
<script src="dist/plugins/ink.iife.min.js"></script>   <!-- window.HicLivePlugins.ink -->
<script type="module">
  const host = await HTMLInCanvasEffects.createHost(canvas, form, { interposer: true });
  const ink = await host.addPlugin(HicLivePlugins.ink.plugin, { intensity: 0.6, flow: 0.5, pigment: 0.7 });
  ink.mountUI(document.getElementById('controls'));                      // three sliders: Stir, Flow, Pigment
  await ink.trigger('demo');                                             // one stroke across the page; 'still' at rest
  await ink.trigger('stir', { point: { x: 120, y: 80 }, angle: 30 });    // a short stroke there
</script>
```

ES modules: `import { plugin } from '@giuseppe-scotto-lavina/html-in-canvas-polyfill/plugins/ink'`.

## Options

| option | default | |
|---|---|---|
| `intensity` | 0.6 | 0 to 1: how hard the pointer pushes the water, the fastest water a hover drives (26 CSS px/s at 1) and how fast a drop spreads; 0 turns the effect off (the output is the source, no pigment) |
| `flow` | 0.5 | 0 to 1: how long swirls live: the flow dies by 1/e in 0.3 s (0), 0.95 s (0.5) or 3 s (1) |
| `heal` | 0.5 | 0 to 1: how fast the page comes home: 1 /s (0), 2.8 /s (0.5) or 8 /s (1) |
| `pigment` | 0.7 | 0 to 1: the density of the ink that a pressed pointer releases; 0 moves the page without any pigment |
| `palette` | `'marbling'` | `'marbling'` (blue, crimson, saffron), `'sumi'` (black with a bronze sheen, vermilion, indigo), `'neon'`, or up to three entries, each a colour (`'#rgb'`, `'#rrggbb'`) or a pair `[ink, sheen]`: the colour of the ink on white paper and the colour of the light it scatters over a dark page |
| `selectText` | `false` | `true`: a pressed pointer that crosses text also selects it, as without the plugin (see Input) |

`mountUI` adds three sliders (`intensity`, `flow`, `pigment`). The gallery passes `meta.defaults`: `{ intensity: 0.6, flow: 0.5, pigment: 0.7 }`.

## Actions

`handle.trigger(action, options)` ([semantics](../../docs/guides/EFFECTS-PLUGINS.md#5-actions-trigger-and-settled)).

| action | options | | resolves with |
|---|---|---|---|
| `demo` | none | one pressed stroke across the page, an S, in 1.3 s (`demoPath`), slow at both ends; then the page heals and the pigment fades | `'still'`, once nothing moves and no pigment is left: 4.4 s at the defaults; `null` when the user takes over during the stroke (a press, a pointer or finger that stirs, `setOptions`: the stroke stops at once and what is there runs out) |
| `stir` | `point`: `{x, y}` in canvas CSS px (default: the centre); `angle`: degrees, 0 to the right, 90 down (default: another direction each time); `strength`: 0.1 to 2 (default 1) | a short pressed stroke through the point: 150 CSS px x `strength` in 0.3 s x √`strength` | `'still'` at rest (about 3.4 s) |
| `clear` | none | the ink is gone at once: flow, displacement and pigment | `'still'`, with the frame that shows the clean page |

`handle.settled()` resolves with `'still'` once nothing moves and no pigment is left (what the pointer stirred counts too). A `point` that is not `{x, y}`, a `strength` or an `angle` that is not a finite number rejects with a `TypeError`; a newer trigger resolves a pending one with `null` (the stroke of a demo stops). The plugin never starts the demo by itself. At `intensity` 0 the actions resolve at once.

## Input

The plugin is `spatial`. `mapInkPoint(state, point)` is the JavaScript twin of the displacement in `ink.wgsl`: the same cubic B-spline over the same 4 x 4 cells of the field, the same clamp to the canvas. It evaluates the uniform block and the field that were uploaded for the presented frame (copied into one of four slots), so it describes exactly those pixels: 0 of 150,000 pixels disagree in the GPU test, and a click reaches the element the user sees mid-swirl. A gesture keeps one frozen mapping. At rest the mapper is the identity. Nothing crosses the walls: a point of the canvas edge maps to the same edge.

- **The pointer** is read through `context.localPoint` (`pointerIn`), so the ink is stirred where the user sees the pointer when another plugin is added after this one.
- **A press on a control** of the page (a field, a button, a slider, a link, a label) is the page's gesture: no drop falls on the field being focused, and a drag that starts there (a slider, a selection in a field) stirs as a hovering pointer does, without pigment.
- **Text selection.** A pressed pointer that moves over the page stirs it; the browser would also select the text it crosses, and the selection would be drawn into the ink. While such a press lasts, a selection that starts outside a text field does not start (`selectstart` is cancelled; this is the plugin's only listener that is not passive). Selections in fields, by the keyboard and without a press are untouched; `selectText: true` turns this off.
- **Touch.** The ink covers the whole canvas and never claims a gesture: its pointer and touch listeners are passive, so a finger keeps scrolling the page. A tap is a drop that blooms. A finger that moves over the canvas stirs and releases pigment along its path (its passive `touchmove` events); a finger that scrolls the page stays over the same spot of the canvas and stirs nothing.

## Reduced motion

With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` (read when the plugin is added, and by the host at every `trigger`) nothing flows and the page never moves: the mapper stays the identity, and there is no sheen. A press leaves a soft tint where it landed (a drop that does not spread, as dense as a stroke), which fades in place over the life of the pigment (2.9 s); a drag draws nothing. `demo` resolves `'still'` at once; `stir` leaves that tint at its point and resolves when it has faded.

## Performance

Measured on 2026-11-11 on an Apple M1 Max (macOS, headless Chrome), at 1280 x 800 CSS px and DPR 2 (2560 x 1600 device px, a grid of 112 x 72 cells, a pigment texture of 1200 x 750), while the demo plays (its stroke crosses the whole canvas, so every pass covers most of it) and while drops fall, one every 0.2 s across the canvas (each spreads for 0.45 s, so two spread at once). `test/ink-gpu.browser.test.mjs` prints these numbers.

- **Zero work at rest.** At rest, and after every stroke once flow, displacement and pigment are gone, the host renders no effect frame (0 in 3 s in the gallery). A source change at rest renders one frame of one pass that reads one texel per pixel.
- **CPU.** The time inside `render()` (the steps of the fluid, the uploads, the encoding of the passes): during the demo at 120 frames per second, one step a frame, 0.70 ms at the mean (p95 0.73 ms, the longest 0.77 ms); at 60 frames per second, two steps a frame, 1.41 ms at the mean (p95 1.59 ms, the longest 1.75 ms). While drops fall: 0.73 ms at the mean at 120 frames per second (p95 0.78 ms), 1.44 ms at 60 (p95 1.51 ms). One step of the 112 x 64 grid of a 1100 x 620 canvas costs 0.43 ms at the mean (p95 0.60 ms) in Node 24 while a stroke stirs it, 0.61 ms (p95 0.67 ms) while four drops spread: about half of it the transport, a fifth the confinement, a sixth the multigrid cycle; the radial flow of a drop adds about 150 cells. A frame at rest costs 0.003 ms. The engine compiles the step before the first stroke: 60 steps of a small fluid while the pipelines compile (about 10 ms), then 30 steps of a fluid of the canvas's size in idle moments of at most 3 ms; without that the first step took 18 ms and the second 5 ms.
- **GPU.** Frames rendered back to back on a device of their own, the time until the GPU is done divided by the frame count (throughput: an upper bound, it includes the submission): during the demo 1.19 ms a frame at 120 frames per second (one pigment step and the picture), 1.59 ms at 60 frames per second (two pigment steps and the picture); while drops fall 0.79 ms and 1.51 ms; against 0.06 ms for a pass that only clears the target and 0.15 ms for the plugin at rest. The sheen costs about 0.15 ms of the demo's frame at 120 frames per second (1.02 ms without it); the wet edge runs only while a drop's pigment lives, and the 20 fine modes of a spreading drop only within its reach for 0.45 s. The picture: per device pixel 16 texel loads of the field (only where the 4 x 4 cells may be non-zero; the second derivatives of the stream function come from the same loads), 4 bilinear taps of the pigment (only inside the rectangle that can hold pigment) and 1 to 6 taps of the page. A pigment step: per texel 16 texel loads of the field for the velocity (32 where the water moves more than a quarter of a CSS px in the step: the midpoint), the radial flow of up to four spreading drops (only within their reach), 10 bilinear taps and 4 loads of the pigment; it runs only while pigment is left, over the rectangle that can hold pigment (it grows no faster than the water moves).
- **Memory.** The field: 8 layers of rgba32float at the grid's size (one per step of a frame; 116 x 68 cells, 1 MB for a 1100 x 620 canvas). The pigment: two rgba16float textures of at most 900,000 texels (14.4 MB together at that size). Four mapper states of 126 KB. The uniform blocks of the pigment steps: 8 of 512 bytes.
- **Allocations.** A frame allocates nothing of its own: the uniform blocks, the pass descriptors, the segment queue and the packed field are reused (the browser makes its pass encoder objects).


## Extended range

With the host's `hdr` option the page and the pigment stay at or under white (the pigment absorbs and scatters the page's own light and adds none), and only the sheen of the moving water goes above it: where the lit surface would pass white, the part above white is added on the shared shoulder of `plugins/_lib/hdr.js` (`hdr_light`), up to `HDR_PEAK`; the shading below white is the same as without `hdr`. The picture pipeline gets `HDR_PEAK` from `context.hdr` (`test/ink.test.mjs` checks it and that `ink.wgsl` holds the shared shoulder). In a standard range the output is what it was before the sheen wherever the water is still.

## Limitations

- The fluid runs on the CPU (so that the input mapper reads exactly what the GPU draws) on cells of about 10 CSS px: swirls smaller than about 3 cells do not form, and the displacement is smooth at that scale. The pigment has about one texel per CSS px and no more detail than that.
- The pigment is carried by a semi-Lagrangian step, which does not conserve its amount exactly: filaments thinner than about 2 texels lose density as they stretch.
- The flow is a closed box with free-slip walls: nothing flows in or out at the canvas edge, and the page's own content (a button, a border) is no obstacle.
- The page heals along straight lines (each point slides home), it does not unwind the way it was wound.
- Pigment fades by its age, not by its density. Ink released onto older ink makes the mixture younger: the age is carried as a mean.
- The spreading of a drop is a prescribed flow (its reach, its duration and the spectrum of its unevenness are set, not the result of a simulation of a falling drop). Its ragged edge lives in the pigment only: the page under the drop moves with the low modes, not with every finger. Its tendrils are pushes on the fluid's grid, at least two cells (20 CSS px) wide.
- The sheen is a reflection of one soft light by the slope of a model surface (Bernoulli's), not of the room or of the page: on a bright page in a standard range it can only shade (light added to white is still white), so a hover over a white page shows little of it.
- Below 15 frames per second the fluid takes at most 8 steps a frame: the motion runs slower than real time instead of jumping.
- With reduced motion a drag draws nothing: only presses leave tints.
- The second largest bundle of the example plugins, after cloth: about a third of it is the two shaders, inlined (minified by the build).
- A resize to another grid starts at rest (a resize by a fraction of a cell keeps the flow); the pigment is dropped whenever its texture changes size.
- WebGPU only (without an adapter `createHost` rejects with a `NotSupportedError`).
