## [0.1.0-preview.11] - 2026-10-28

Smaller downloads, same library. The minified bundles get a second minification pass: the core is 112,066 bytes gzipped instead of 117,183 (95,252 with brotli, as jsDelivr serves it, instead of 100,388). Behaviour is unchanged: the browser gates, the WPT subject run and the examples give the same results on the new minified core as on the previous one, and its performance is within measurement noise.

### Changed

- Smaller minified bundles: every `.min.js` classic bundle (the core, effects, native bridge, engines, GPU, worker and each plugin) now gets a second pass by Terser after esbuild, with options that change no behaviour (no unsafe transform, no property mangling, ASCII output). The minified core shrinks from 335,744 to 331,972 bytes, 117,183 to 112,066 gzipped (-4.4 %) and 100,388 to 95,252 with brotli; all minified bundles together lose 7,756 bytes gzipped (-3.4 %). The readable `.js` bundles and the ES modules are unchanged. Terser is a devDependency; the package still has no runtime dependencies.

## [0.1.0-preview.10] - 2026-10-27

Smoother scrolling on iPhone, and in Firefox where it can be made exact. On iOS, one empty drawable canvas anywhere on a page (a canvas whose content is added later) kept the scroll compositor from running for every canvas of the page and kept the scheduler busy on an idle page; this is fixed (iOS Simulator: 58 canvas updates per second instead of 29-30 on the scroll-and-resize example with such a canvas, 210-212 composed steps instead of 0). Firefox now composes scroll steps at a device pixel ratio of 1, for square scrollers whose content has no rounded corners, dotted lines, transforms, images, SVG or form controls (headless Firefox: 110-117 updates per second instead of 39-71); elsewhere it still rebuilds. The field test gains a scroll smoothness step, so testers on real phones and PCs can send back comparable numbers.

### Added

- Field test: a "Scroll smoothness" step, so that a tester's phone or PC reports how smoothly a long list drawn into a canvas scrolls. Two lists with sticky headers and rows of fractional height in one canvas (a rounded opaque one and a square transparent one) are scrolled three ways: by a scripted momentum scroll (the baseline), by jumps whose first frame is compared with the settled canvas, and by the tester's own flicks (or wheel / touchpad), with a countdown. It reports the metrics of `test/bench/scroll-jank.mjs` and `scroll-jank-ios.mjs` (canvas updates per second, stutters, the longest gap, moves not painted, the first-step lag, the scroll compositor's counters, clone and decode times) under the report key `scrollSmoothness`, with the device's pixel ratio, raster scale and frame interval, and a green / amber / red verdict per part whose thresholds come from those benches (`docs/guides/FIELD-TEST.md`). Every wait is bounded, the step and its touch part can be skipped, and it needs no tab capture and no WebGPU.
- `scripts/check-field-test.mjs`: the step in the `clean` and `dpr125` runs, and two positive controls, `smooth-drop` (a canvas that updates in every other frame must be red) and `smooth-shift` (a first frame 1 px off must be red). `test/bench/field-smoothness-ios.mjs` runs the step in the iOS Simulator. `scripts/field-report.mjs` prints it.
- G-xbrowser: the Firefox scenario `scrollCompose`, which compares every composed scroll frame with the settled canvas at the same offset (10 jumps) and checks that what Gecko must not compose is refused; its control serves mutant cores. It runs in the macOS CI job with the other Firefox scenarios. The Firefox launcher of the test harness takes a headless option (`HIC_FIREFOX_HEADLESS=1`).

### Changed

- The core bundle grows to 335,744 bytes minified, 117,183 gzipped (+1,325 / +477 bytes for the scroll compositor on Gecko, +338 / +127 bytes for the empty-canvas and refusal fixes).

### Fixed

- Field test: on iOS the page kept the scroll compositor from running. The effect step's canvas waited on the page as an empty drawable canvas (no drawable child yet), and an empty drawable canvas on a page stops the compositor for every canvas of the page in Mobile Safari (measured in the iOS Simulator with the scroll-and-resize example: 0-1 composed scroll steps instead of about 215; desktop Safari 27 was not affected in our runs; the polyfill itself is fixed, see the next entry). The step canvases are now created by their steps. A skipped step that finished later no longer scrolls the page to the top under the next step.
- Scroll in Firefox: inner scrolling is drawn from cached pages of the scroller, as in Chrome and Safari, where Firefox paints them exactly: at a device pixel ratio of 1, in a scroller with square corners, for content without rounded corners, dotted lines, transforms, images, SVG or form controls. Every scroll step was a full rebuild of the snapshot in Firefox. Firefox renders a scroll offset in a snapshot at its exact value and snaps each edge on its own, so only whole-pixel offsets are composed. Its snapshots lay out text and borders as on a 1x display at every ratio, so another ratio is refused. A clip edge crossing a rounded corner changes how Firefox paints it (up to 159 of 255 levels), so such content is refused too. Headless Firefox 157 at 1x, scripted scroll: 110-117 canvas updates per second instead of 39-71 on composable lists (300 and 2000 rows), 1-3 stutters per run instead of 25-55; with 8 ms of extra work per frame 105-111 instead of 52-68. Every composed frame equals the settled canvas. Still rebuilt in Firefox: the scroll-and-resize example (checkboxes in its rows), the F2 fixture (rounded badges, fields), any page at a ratio other than 1 (most Macs and many Windows laptops), classic scrollbars and a scroller with rounded corners (Firefox and the canvas antialias its curve differently).
- Scroll compositor: one empty drawable canvas on a page (a `<canvas content="drawable">` with no drawable child yet, hidden or not) stopped the scroll compositor for every canvas of the page, and kept the scheduler ticking at the display rate on an idle page. Such a canvas fires no paint, as natively, so the scheduler took it for a canvas whose first snapshot was still due and built inside the frame in every tick it took part in. Side builds (scroll plans and pages) never run inside the frame, and the work they left made every canvas tick again, so they never ran at all. The same happened with a drawable added after the mount of a canvas that never painted, when that drawable had no box (zero size, hidden) or its first snapshot never committed. A canvas now builds its first snapshot inside the frame only while it has a drawable and for at most `paintGateMaxWaitMs` (400 ms) after it got one. Measured in the iOS Simulator (iPhone 17 Pro, iOS 26.4), scroll-and-resize example with an empty canvas added, scripted scroll: 0 composed steps of 229 before, 210-212 after; 29-30 canvas updates per second before, 58 after; 116-118 moves left unpainted before, 7-9 after; idle ticks 61 per second before, 0 after. The composed frames equal the settled canvas (oracle: worst diff 0 %). On the field test's scroll step with its canvases created up front: 0 composed steps before, 222 of 230 after, and 12 of 12 jumps composed. Headless Chrome was not affected (1x and 6x CPU throttling, the example and a list 8 times longer): the loop needs a post-frame pump that leaves side builds for the next one, which never happened there. Desktop Safari 27 on a MacBook Pro (DPR 2) was not affected either: with an empty canvas added, the inbox and both fixture lists composed 217-222 of 229 steps at 58-59 updates per second, with 0.1.0-preview.9 as with this version, and every composed jump equalled the settled canvas. Regression checks: scheduler unit tests (an empty canvas; a drawable added later without a box; one that never commits), and in G-mobile `scrollCompose` a scripted scroll beside an empty canvas, controlled by a mutant core.
- Scroll compositor: a scroller refused while part of its port was off-screen was planned again only after newer damage of its drawable. It is now also planned again once it has moved on screen (a page scroll brought it into view), at most once a second. Before, the list's own first scroll step that was not composed counted as such damage, so the gain is the start of a gesture at most (iOS Simulator, a list refused partly off-screen, then scrolled into view and scrolled again for 40 steps: 33-37 composed before, 36-37 after).

## [0.1.0-preview.9] - 2026-10-27

Smooth scrolling inside a drawable on Safari and iOS. The scroll compositor, which draws each step of an inner scroller from cached pages instead of rebuilding the snapshot, now runs on WebKit as well as on Chromium (Gecko still rebuilds every step): on WebKit's 1 css px layout grid, with a rounded scrollport composed only over an opaque scroller background. Under a scripted 60 Hz scroll in desktop Safari 27 at DPR 2, the canvas updates per second rose from about 30 to 59 on the scroll-and-resize example and from about 25 to 59 on the F2 scroll fixture, and the stutters fell from about 30 to 0-1 per run; on the iOS Simulator (iPhone 17 Pro, DPR 3) the example rose from about 30 to 58. The Simulator runs on the Mac's CPU, so a phone is slower. The rows left out of a snapshot are now stood in for at their exact layout length, so a composed scroll frame equals the settled picture (pixel-equal to the settled frame on all 4 pages tested in desktop Safari 27, and on the fixtures measured in headless Chrome at DPR 2 and 3) where rows of fractional height jumped by a device pixel when the scroll ended. Not fixed: on desktops with a device pixel ratio above 2.5 the snapshot is rasterised at 2.5 (`maxDpr`), and composed frames there can still drift slightly until the scroll settles. The minified core grows to 334,080 bytes (116,578 gzipped).

### Added

- G-scroll-cadence: a case of rows of fractional height (`fracrows`: 36.6 px rows, so pages and tiles are pruned), and the mutant `spacer-length-nearest` that it catches (48 mutants). G-mobile: the iOS scenario `scrollCompose`, which compares every composed scroll frame with the settled canvas at the same offset at DPR 3; its control serves two mutant cores. `test/bench/scroll-jank.mjs --dpr`.

### Changed

- The core bundle grows to 334,080 bytes minified, 116,578 gzipped (333,701 / 116,437 with the scroll compositor on WebKit; 332,769 / 116,096 in 0.1.0-preview.8): +932 / +341 bytes for the scroll compositor on WebKit and +379 / +141 bytes for the exact stand-in lengths below (measured in `scripts/check-bundle.mjs`).

### Fixed

- Scroll on iOS and Safari: scrolling inside a drawable no longer stutters every few frames. Every scroll step was a full rebuild of the snapshot on WebKit (the scroll compositor ran on Chromium only), about 11 ms per step on the iOS Simulator and more on a phone. The compositor now runs on WebKit too: a step is drawn from cached pages of the scroller in the step's own frame. It had been held back because its frames came out one device pixel off on an iPhone: WebKit lays the snapshot out at css scale and snaps every edge and scroll offset to whole css pixels before scaling it, and the compositor snapped to device pixels. It now snaps where WebKit does, and a rounded scroller is blended into its corners in a way WebKit's canvas keeps opaque. Desktop Safari 27 at DPR 2 under a scripted 60 Hz scroll: about 59 canvas updates per second instead of about 30 on the scroll-and-resize example and about 25 on the F2 scroll fixture, 0-1 stutters per run instead of about 30. Measured on the iOS Simulator (which runs on the Mac's CPU, so a phone is slower) with the scroll-and-resize example at DPR 3: 58 canvas updates per second instead of 30, 1 stutter per run instead of 5-7; with 8 ms of extra work per frame 57 instead of 23 per second, 2-5 stutters instead of 47-50. Still rebuilt on WebKit: a scroller with a classic scrollbar (desktop Safari with "always show scroll bars"), a rounded scroller with a translucent background, and a snapshot raster at a fractional scale. A new mobile gate scenario checks every composed frame against the exact snapshot at DPR 3.
- Scroll: a list of rows of fractional height (such as 36.6 px) no longer jumps by a device pixel when a composed scroll settles. The rows above the visible part are left out of a snapshot and stand-ins of the same height take their place; the stand-in's height was written rounded to the nearest 0.001 px, and the browser truncates a length to its layout units (1/64 of a device pixel) and keeps 6 significant digits of a length, so a stand-in was often one layout unit short. The pages of the scroll compositor and the settled snapshot leave out different rows, so the same row landed a pixel apart (headless Chrome, up to 5,665 pixels in a composed frame at DPR 3 and 3,153 at DPR 2; 0 after). Not fixed: where the snapshot is rasterised at a lower scale than the screen's (above `maxDpr`, 2.5 on desktops; a surface over its pixel budget), border widths snap differently in the snapshot and composed frames can still drift from the settled one by a few device pixels until the scroll settles.

## [0.1.0-preview.8] - 2026-10-27

A bug fix release for scrolling. A long composed scroll (about 3 s of uninterrupted wheel, trackpad or fling scrolling over a list drawn from cached pages) made the polyfill uninstall itself, leaving the canvas frozen until the page was reloaded; in Chrome and Edge (the only engines where scroll steps are composed), any drawn scroller that the compositor accepts could hit it. The first wheel or trackpad gesture over a drawn scroller no longer stutters, and a scroller of fractional width is composed from cached pages again instead of rebuilt at every step. The headed browser suites now run on GitHub-hosted macOS runners. The minified core grows to 332,769 bytes (116,096 gzipped).

### Added

- CI on GitHub-hosted macOS runners (`.github/workflows/ci-macos.yml`): 10 of the 15 headed Chrome gates with their positive controls, the mutation suite (one mutant per gate on pull requests; the 24 mutants whose gates run there nightly and on release tags, split over four machines with the new `--shard=k/n`), the examples and documentation smoke pages that render no WebGPU, the Pages site, framework apps, hang stress and platform probe, and G-xbrowser on Firefox (all scenarios but scroll). Every run lists, as warnings, what stays local and why (`test/ci/macos-plan.mjs`): the suites that need a 120 Hz display or a fast machine for throughput checks (G-regress-paint), G-scroll-inner (an unexplained page-scroll difference seen there), WebGPU (the runner's virtual GPU presents no continuous effect frames and lost its device once), the field test (its tab capture), the WPT gate, Safari 27 and Technology Preview, and the phone simulators. `npm run test:local` runs that rest on a Mac.
- `scripts/check-examples.mjs --only` takes `smoke:<name>` to run single documentation smoke pages.
- `HIC_FIREFOX_PREFS` (JSON) adds Firefox prefs for a machine's G-xbrowser runs.
- Scroll jank measurements (reported data, no thresholds): `test/bench/scroll-jank.mjs` (headed Chrome, trusted wheel and touch streams, CPU throttling) and `test/bench/scroll-jank-ios.mjs` (Mobile Safari on the iOS Simulator); see `docs/TEST-PLAN.md`.
- `HIC_GATE_DPR` gives the gates without a device pixel ratio of their own a forced ratio (the runner's display is 1x; CI uses 2, the ratio of the screens the thresholds were set on).

### Changed

- The core bundle grows to 332,769 bytes minified, 116,096 gzipped (was 332,499 / 115,986), for the scroll fixes below (measured in `scripts/check-bundle.mjs`).

### Fixed

- **Serious:** a long scroll of a list drawn from cached pages uninstalled the polyfill. Composed scroll steps were invisible to the runaway guard (which uninstalls the polyfill when the pipeline keeps committing with nothing on the page asking for it), so about 3 s of uninterrupted composed scrolling (a long wheel or trackpad scroll, a fling) tripped it: the polyfill logged a console error, uninstalled itself and stayed uninstalled, so the canvas kept its last picture and stopped following the page until it was reloaded. Reproduced on 0.1.0-preview.7 with a 3.4 s wheel scroll of a 2000-row list in headed Chrome. Each composed scroll step now counts as input for the guard, as a scroll event always did.
- Scroll: a scroller of fractional width (a grid or flex track, such as the list of the scroll-and-resize example) is composed from cached pages again. The compositor took the scrollport's size from `clientWidth` / `clientHeight`, which are rounded to whole pixels: every sticky header was one device pixel wider than that port and the scroller was refused (and at DPR 1.25 the rounding showed a scrollbar gutter of -1 px, also refused), so each scroll step rebuilt the whole list. Touch scrolling of that example without a hovering pointer (as on a phone), headed Chrome 120 Hz: canvas updates during the scroll 65 -> 115 per second at full CPU speed and 17 -> 75 with the CPU slowed 4x.
- Scroll: the first wheel or trackpad gesture over a drawn scroller no longer starts with a stutter. The plan and the first cached pages of a scroller under the pointer were meant to be built while the pointer rests, but nothing woke the scheduler, so they waited for the first scroll event: that gesture began with a whole-scroller build inside its first frame and the planning and page builds in the frames after it. Measured (headed Chrome, 120 Hz, wheel and touch streams on four lists, CPU throttled 1x/4x/6x, 24 runs): canvas update gaps longer than 2.5 frames while the list moved 82 -> 0, main-thread tasks over 16.7 ms during the gestures 262 -> 141.
- Test harness: launching Firefox for G-xbrowser no longer waits forever when macOS shows a dialog about the application (the first-launch prompt of a quarantined download); it fails after 30 s and names the cause. A tab that closes between being listed and being attached is replaced by a new one instead of failing the run.
- G-scroll-inner reads the page scroll of each tab once its smooth scroll has ended, while that tab is in front: a fixed 400 ms wait read the plain-DOM twin frozen part-way (a background tab does not advance the animation), seen once on the slower hosted macOS runner.
- Field test page: starting the tab capture can no longer wait forever for its first frame (the page now changes before the capture starts, and a capture that delivers no frame within 10 s is reported as an error).

## [0.1.0-preview.7] - 2026-10-26

Conformance with the official WPT HTML-in-Canvas tests at the pinned revision (2026-10-18) rises to 206 of 237 files and 324 of 369 subtests (was 198 and 316), with no polyfill bug open. Animated GIF, APNG, WebP and AVIF images inside a drawable now play, decoded from the bytes the polyfill may already read. Rendering is closer to native for external SVG `<use>` documents, clip paths defined outside the drawable, a video whose poster fails to load, the macOS overlay thumb of a listbox `<select>` and date / time fields at a fractional device pixel ratio. The browser test harness now tells a disturbed machine (another application in front, a moving mouse pointer) apart from a product failure. The minified core grows to 332,499 bytes (115,986 gzipped).

### Added

- An animated GIF, APNG, WebP or AVIF `<img>` inside a drawable is played: each new frame of the image is drawn and fires a `paint` event, as natively (it showed its first frame forever). Frames are decoded with `ImageDecoder` from the same bytes the polyfill already inlines, so a cross-origin image without CORS is never read; an image plays only while a canvas that shows it can be seen, at most at that canvas's frame cap and build rate, and decoded frames are kept in a bounded cache. Safari 27 (no `ImageDecoder`) and pages that are not a secure context show the first frame, with one console message. Animated CSS images (`background-image` and the like) still show their first frame.

### Changed

- The official WPT HTML-in-Canvas tests at the same revision (2026-10-18): the polyfill passes 206 of 237 files and 324 of 369 subtests (was 198 and 316); native Chrome Canary 158.0.8112.1, the build of the pinned tests, passes 222 and 357 in every full run (the Canary 158.0.8122.1 that ran this time fails 10 filter and `feImage` subtests of that revision, which are judged against the 158.0.8112.1 runs: `docs/CONFORMANCE.md`, oracle drift). Every remaining difference is classified in `docs/CONFORMANCE.md`: 32 subtests that a userland polyfill cannot pass, 5 documented deviations (the canvas-origin placement of a nested drawable, the decoration edge of a `::selection` and a CSS Custom Highlight), 8 that the native build fails too.
- The core bundle grows to 332,499 bytes minified, 115,986 gzipped (was 315,946 / 109,908), for the changes below (each measured in `scripts/check-bundle.mjs`).
- Test harness: intermittent gate failures traced to the shared machine, not to the polyfill, are now prevented where they can be and otherwise reported as environment problems instead of product failures. A headed Chrome on macOS delivers the resting OS pointer to the page at every clipped screenshot and tab activation, so a pointer left over the test window moved `:hover` behind the gate (G-scroll-cadence's plain-DOM twin lost its hovered row, one row band 6.3 % different, at both DPRs): the harness moves the pointer out of the browser window at launch, every gate page records pointer input the gate did not send and such a run is `NOT USABLE`, and G-scroll-cadence checks that each twin screenshot shows the row it was given. In the cross-browser gate, a scenario during which another application came to the front (a browser that is not the active application swallows trusted clicks, Safari's clickEffect, and cannot activate a popup, Firefox's inactive) runs again and then fails as an environment problem; so does one during which the mouse pointer moved (real pointer input changed hover and press looks in Firefox's interaction scenario), and the pointer is moved off the browser window before each scenario.
- Gate runners: a gate process that prints no result is reported as not run with the reason (exit code, signal, runner timeout) instead of crashing the mutant suite, and a gate still running after 10 minutes reports itself with the calls it was waiting for (`NOT USABLE` behind a locked screen).

### Fixed

- An attribute change on an ancestor of the canvas no longer fires a `paint` event when it cannot change what the drawables paint (a class that only restyles that ancestor, an attribute no rule tests): only a class, id or attribute that a style rule tests above its subject, `lang` / `dir`, or a change of the values the canvas passes on (its inherited and custom properties) repaints the canvas. Every such change used to rebuild every tile.
- `getSelection().selectAllChildren(canvas)` and `range.selectNodeContents(canvas)` select the drawables of a managed canvas, as natively; they selected nothing, since the canvas's children live in its layer.
- The focus ring of a focused image-map `<area>` is drawn over the `<img>` of a drawable that uses its map (rect, circle and default shapes), in the area's outline colour, never its `:visited` one.
- A light-DOM drawable slotted into a `content=drawable` canvas that lives in a shadow tree (`<canvas content=drawable><slot>`) is a drawable of that canvas, as in the flat tree; drawing it threw `InvalidStateError`. Changes inside it repaint it.
- `getElementTransform`, `updateElementGeometry` and `clearElementGeometry` accept an SVG element inside an `<svg>` (`<rect drawable>`) and keep its canvas transform, as natively; they threw `HicUnsupportedError`. Capturing or drawing it is still refused.
- The references inside a same-origin external SVG document drawn by `<use href="icons.svg#a">` resolve against that document, as natively: its gradients and patterns are ignored with their fallback colour kept (a `fill="url(#g)"` there drew the page's `#g` when the drawable had one), its masks mask their element away, its clip paths and filters apply (they were dropped), and its own `<style>` rules style it (they were ignored, or, inside the target, styled the page too). Its ids and classes are renamed in the snapshot, so the page's references, class and id rules never reach it and its never reach the page. Measured against native Chrome Canary 158.
- A `clip-path: url(#id)` (CSS or the SVG attribute) whose `<clipPath>` is outside the drawable clips, as natively; the element was drawn unclipped. A gradient, pattern or marker inside a `<use>` target copied from outside the drawable is ignored, and the target's own `filter` attributes do not apply, as natively; they were drawn.
- With macOS overlay scrollbars, a scrollable listbox `<select>` (`size` > 1 or `multiple`) shows Chrome's overlay thumb, as Chrome draws it in a drawable: 7 px wide at rest, black at 50 % over a light background and white over a dark one, over the selected option. It was missing (5 % of the listbox's pixels). Within 0.13 % of the page at DPR 1 and 0.03 % at DPR 2; not painted for `scrollbar-width: thin` or `scrollbar-color`.
- A date or time field (`type=date`, `time`, `datetime-local`, `month`, `week`) keeps its width at a fractional device pixel ratio: the snapshot set the text of its fields 1-2 device pixels narrower than the page at DPR 1.25, 1.5 and 2, which moved every control after it in its line (the F6 listboxes after a time field failed the controls bounds, 12-19 % of their pixels over 16).
- A `<video>` whose poster fails to load (404, not an image, a cross-origin image without CORS) draws nothing until it plays, as natively and in the page; it showed its first frame.

## [0.1.0-preview.6] - 2026-10-22

The conformance gate now runs the official WPT HTML-in-Canvas tests at their newest revision (2026-10-18): the polyfill passes 198 of 237 files and 316 of 369 subtests, native Chrome Canary 158.0.8112.1 225 and 357, and no polyfill bug is open. Content that read-back-allowed rendering hides is now hidden as natively: an unreadable mask image masks its element away, a cross-origin video is an invisible box, and an SVG `<use>` draws only same-origin documents. **Breaking:** the two-argument `getElementTransform(element, drawTransform)` and `OffscreenCanvas.getElementTransform`, removed from the specification on 2026-10-09, are gone; see Removed for the migration.

### Changed

- The official WPT HTML-in-Canvas tests are pinned at the newest commit that touches them, `850b36a5` (2026-10-18; was `d9b949e5`, 2026-10-07): 237 files and 369 subtests (was 230 and 375). The polyfill passes 198 files and 316 subtests (was 182 and 307), native Chrome Canary 158.0.8112.1 225 and 357, Chrome without the polyfill 10 and 19, all vacuously. The oracle is the Chrome build whose imported copy of the tests is closest to the pinned commit (306 of 309 files byte-identical; the other 3 carry assertions Chromium has not exported to WPT yet), and its baseline comes from three full runs. The `ElementImage` size tests (`captureElementImage`, `element-image-fractional-size`, `offscreenCanvas-drawElementImage`) now pass: they test `getElementImageDefaultSize`, which the polyfill already followed. The polyfill bugs that the new tests and resources exposed (cross-origin masks, a same-origin SVG `<use>` of an external document, a popover frame, the poster of a paused video, an iframe moved into a drawable) are fixed (below); no polyfill bug is open, and every remaining difference has a written reason in `docs/CONFORMANCE.md`.
- The WPT runner serves every image and video the tests load, as the upstream server does: five images and a video were missing and answered 404, so the tests that load them passed or failed for the wrong reason (`privacy/video-ignored` passed only because its video did not load), and the gate now fails on any referenced file that is not vendored. It also applies the `?pipe=header(Name,value)` responses the CORS test needs.

### Removed

- **Breaking:** the two-argument `canvas.getElementTransform(element, drawTransform)` and `OffscreenCanvas.getElementTransform(element, drawTransform)` (on the page and in the worker bundle). They belonged to the 2026-10-07 draft; the specification and the official tests removed them on 2026-10-09 (WPT `b59184b6`) and native Chrome no longer has them. The polyfill kept them as deprecated overloads while its conformance gate ran the earlier WPT revision. `canvas.getElementTransform(element)` is unchanged and now ignores a second argument, as WebIDL does, so a call that still passes one returns the stored canvas transform instead of the matrix a draw would store, and the first such call logs one console warning. Instead: draw the element with `drawElementImage` (or call `updateElementGeometry`) and read `canvas.getElementTransform(element)`, or compute the matrix in the page as `S^-1 * D * S` with `D` the draw transform and `S` the scale from css px to the canvas grid (`docs/API-CONTRACT.md` 4.7). On an `OffscreenCanvas`, keep the matrix of your own `drawElementImage` calls; its element geometry reaches the page's canvas, where `getElementTransform(element)` reads it.

### Fixed

- A `mask-image` (or `mask`, `-webkit-mask`) whose image the polyfill may not read, a cross-origin image without CORS or one that fails to load, masks its element away, as CSS Masking requires for an image that cannot be displayed and as the native implementation does in read-back-allowed rendering; the element was drawn unmasked. On SVG content (an element inside an `<svg>`) such a mask is ignored and the element drawn unmasked, as natively. A mask image that is still loading keeps a box masked away until it lands. WPT `privacy/box-mask-png-ignored`, `box-mask-svg-ignored`, `fragment-mask-png-ignored`, `fragment-mask-svg-ignored`, `svg/svg-mask-on-svg-content-ignored`.
- An SVG `<use>` that refers to another document (`<use href="icons.svg#a">`, or the whole document without a fragment) is drawn when that document is same-origin, with the references inside it resolved against that document; it drew nothing, since the snapshot cannot load external resources. A cross-origin one draws nothing, as natively, and so does a same-origin URL that redirects to another origin, even one that allows a CORS read: SVG documents are fetched in same-origin mode, as the platform fetches an external `<use>`. WPT `privacy/svg/svg-use-ignored`.
- A `<video>` with a poster that has loaded but never played shows its poster, as HTML requires while its show-poster flag is set; it showed the first frame. A video whose media data is cross-origin is an invisible box (it keeps its place and paints nothing, its poster included), as read-back-allowed rendering requires; it was a hatched placeholder. Still reported. WPT `privacy/video-ignored` (with the test's `media/white.webm`, which the pinned copy lacks).
- A popover inside a drawable that is shown or hidden (`showPopover()`, `hidePopover()`, `popovertarget`) repaints its drawable: these change no attribute, so the canvas kept the snapshot taken before the toggle (while open the popover is in the top layer and left out, as natively; once hidden, an author `display` draws it in place again). A popover inside an open shadow root is covered too, and so is an image that finishes loading there: neither event leaves its shadow tree. WPT `privacy/popover-show-hide-background-image-ignored`.
- A node moved into a canvas with `moveBefore()` keeps its state as natively: `canvas.moveBefore()` is routed to the canvas's live layer, and a node that reached the canvas through the native method moves on into the layer with `moveBefore()` instead of a remove-and-insert, so an `<iframe>` keeps its document (it reloaded, and the canvas showed its blank initial document in 2 of 6 loads) and focus, animations and playing media are kept. WPT `privacy/box-reflect-images-in-iframe-moved-ignored`.

## [0.1.0-preview.5] - 2026-10-22

Conformance rises to 182 of 230 official WPT files and 307 of 375 subtests (was 145 and 253): same-origin frames, videos, `mix-blend-mode`, `backdrop-filter`, declarative shadow roots and `::selection` decorations are drawn as natively, and an `ElementImage` can be drawn in a dedicated worker. ### Added

- An `ElementImage` can be posted to a dedicated worker and drawn there, as natively: load the new worker bundle at the top of the worker (`importScripts('hic-live-worker.iife.min.js')`, or `import '@giuseppe-scotto-lavina/html-in-canvas-polyfill/worker'` in a module worker). The page sends the snapshot's pixels; in the worker `drawElementImage` on the OffscreenCanvas of its canvas, `getElementImageDefaultSize`, `updateElementGeometry` and `clearElementGeometry` work, and the geometry of the worker's draws is applied on the page with one `elementgeometryupdate` per worker turn. Another canvas's or a standalone OffscreenCanvas throws `InvalidStateError`. The page applies the geometry a worker sends only when it is well-formed and names an element whose image was posted to that same worker. Posting one through a MessagePort or BroadcastChannel still throws `HicUnsupportedError('worker-element-image')`. - The effects extension runs over a browser's own HTML-in-Canvas implementation (mode `native`), with the same bundles and the same API: `HTMLInCanvasEffects.createHost` reads the source with the standard `GPUQueue.drawElementImageToTexture` in the canvas's `paint` event, renders one frame per display frame within the frame cap, and remaps pointer input by moving the standard hit-test geometry (`updateElementGeometry`) under the pointer, so the browser's own hover, `:active`, cursor, focus, caret and selection follow the element the user sees. The off-screen pause, plugin actions and device-loss recovery work there too. Measured on Chrome Canary 158 with `--enable-blink-features=CanvasDrawElement`: with no plugin the presented frame equals the browser's 2D `drawElementImage` within 1 level; trusted clicks through the CRT plugin at full curvature and the water plugin at full intensity reach the field the user sees in 45 of 45 and 42 of 42 places where the DOM position would miss. A native implementation without these members (the earlier `layoutsubtree` draft) makes `createHost` reject loudly. Details: `docs/guides/EFFECTS-PLUGINS.md`, `docs/API-CONTRACT.md` 7.6.
- `install()` defines the extension property `canvas.frameCap` in the native modes too (removed by `uninstall()`); nothing of the standard surface is patched there.
- `node scripts/build.mjs --analyze` prints where the bytes of the minified core come from, by module group and module; `docs/BUNDLES.md` explains why the core has its size and which split was measured and not adopted.
- `npm run test:native-effects`: gates G-native-effects and G-native-interposer (headed, with positive controls), the water and disintegrate gallery tests and `examples/effect-plugin`, all on Chrome Canary over its native implementation; skipped with a loud message where Canary is not installed. `npm run test:gates:all` runs it.

### Changed

- The examples (`examples/plugins/`, `examples/effect-plugin/`) run the effects over a native implementation instead of falling back to 2D.
- Over a native implementation the effects add one element to the page, `<hic-hover-sync>` (one per document, `aria-hidden`, without a box; removed with the last host), whose layout makes the browser recompute hover and the cursor after the interposer moved the hit-test geometry. What changes at each pointer move is inside its closed shadow root, so the page's `MutationObserver`s see only its insertion and removal (`docs/API-CONTRACT.md` 7.6).

### Fixed

- Content the browser itself leaves out of a canvas is now drawn as the browser draws it, measured against the native implementation, instead of as a hatched placeholder: a cross-origin `<img>` or `<picture>` keeps its box (border, background) without the picture, a cross-origin SVG `<image>` draws nothing, an `<iframe>` draws its own box without the document, and an `<object>` or `<embed>` draws the image it shows when that image is readable. The console message and the `report()` entry are unchanged. WPT `privacy/picture-images-ignored`, `embed-image-ignored`, `object-image-ignored`.
- Same-origin iframes (and `<object>` documents) inside a drawable are drawn, as natively: the frame's document is captured with its own CSS (without its `:visited` rules, as for the page's own CSS), its readable images and fonts, its form state and its nested frames, its cross-origin content is left out, and the drawable repaints when that document changes or the frame loads another one. Cross-origin and sandboxed frames draw their own box without the document, are reported, and are no longer reported while they are still loading. WPT `iframe`, `iframe-local`, `privacy/iframes-ignored`, `iframe-sandbox-ignored`, the six `background-image-*-in-iframe-*` and the five `pseudos/before-*-iframe-*` tests, `subframe-cross-origin-frame` (the test runner left the frames of a cross-site frame paused).
- Canvases in a declarative shadow root (`<template shadowrootmode="open">`, which the parser attaches without `attachShadow`) were refused as if their root were closed; open roots are now found when the document is parsed or when one of their canvases is used. Paint events of canvases in different trees (shadow roots, slots, fallback content) fire in reverse shadow-including tree order, as the standard requires; the order across trees was arbitrary. WPT `onpaint-post-tree-order-shadow-dom`.
- An `<img>` inside a drawable whose source is chosen after the snapshot (a `<picture>` source, `srcset`, lazy loading) is drawn once it loads: the drawable repaints when an image finishes loading. WPT `privacy/picture-images-ignored` showed the earlier snapshot in 1 of 3 cold loads.
- A playing `<video>` inside a drawable repaints its drawable at every new video frame (one `paint` per frame, as natively); it showed the frame of the last snapshot until something else changed. WPT `video`, `offscreen/video-worker`.
- Selected page text is drawn with the `text-decoration` and `text-shadow` of an author `::selection` rule, as natively; they were left out. WPT `privacy/selection-alone-with-decorations-painted` goes from 354 to 8 differing pixels (the native decoration ends at the whole-pixel highlight rect).
- A drawable with its own `mix-blend-mode` is blended with what the canvas already holds when it is drawn, and its own `backdrop-filter` filters the canvas content under its border box, as natively; it was drawn over the content unchanged. WPT `css-blend-mode-over-content`, `css-blend-mode-over-both`, `css-backdrop-filter`.
- SVG filters and masks in drawables follow the native read-back rules: an `feImage` inside the drawable is inlined (it drew nothing), a filter whose `feImage` is cross-origin without CORS (or has a URL that does not parse, which made the whole snapshot a placeholder) is not applied at all, a same-origin `feImage` that fails to load keeps the filter with an empty image, and a mask defined outside the drawable masks its element away. WPT `privacy/filtered-div-multi-filters-ignored`, `filtered-div-ref-svg-feimage-ignored`, `filtered-div-ref-svg-mixed-ignored`, `filtered-svg-ref-svg-feimage-ignored`, `svg/svg-pattern-with-feImage-cross-origin-image`, `svg/svg-mask-filter`, `svg/svg-mask-nested-filter`, `svg/svg-mask-visited`.

## [0.1.0-preview.2] - 2026-10-19

A performance release. Scrolling inside a drawable is presented at the display rate in Chrome, effects do no work while their canvas cannot be seen, and snapshot builds read less of the live page. The API is unchanged; `host.stats()` of the effect host gains three fields.

### Performance

- Scrolling inside a drawable is presented at the display rate in Chrome. A live scroller under the pointer (or one that scrolls) is given an invisible main-thread repaint reason, so Chrome's compositor waits for the frame that redraws the canvas instead of delivering the scroll offset in every second frame. Measured at 120 Hz with a trusted wheel stream: a 300-row list from 61 to 113 canvas updates per second (native: 116), with a `:hover` style on its rows from 61 to 61-109 (those steps are built, each hovered row being a change, as often as the main-thread budget allows), rounded corners from 60 to 104, 2000 rows from 4 to 95, the F2 fixture from 24 to 81; main-thread time of the plain list from 36 % to 24 % of the stream. The scroller's look is untouched: its attribute and rule never reach a snapshot, an author transition on `box-shadow` sees no transition from it, and a scroller whose shadow or background is animated, a shadow host and a slotted scroller are left alone (`docs/API-CONTRACT.md` 4.9). Firefox and Safari are not changed by it.
- The scroll compositor plans a scroller when the pointer reaches it (or at its first scroll event), so the first gesture is composed; it keeps its cached pages when something elsewhere in the drawable changes (a counter beside the list), marks the part of the pages a change inside the list touches as stale until the next tile repairs it, composes rounded scrollports (also with a classic scrollbar) inside their rounded clip, and plans scrollers of up to 20,000 elements in steps. Its page builds measure the truncation of a long list once per scroller instead of once per page (2000 rows: page builds p50 6.5 to 5.2 ms).
- `options.maxSnapshotFps` and the `nodeBudget.hard` interval no longer hold the build of a scroller's own scroll damage (rows hovered by a still pointer may ride along); the framecap and the main-thread budget still do. Every other change keeps both limits (`docs/API-CONTRACT.md` 7.1). A 2000-row list now follows every wheel step in Firefox and Safari too (before: 3 to 5 updates per stream of about 60 steps).
- `:hover` rules that only change paint (colours, backgrounds, shadows, outlines, opacity) damage the hovered element alone, also the first time it is hovered.
- The effect host does no work for a canvas nobody can see: while the canvas is more than half a viewport (or half its scroll container) out of view, or the document is hidden, it renders and uploads nothing, `continuous` plugins included (gallery with water: renderer + GPU process CPU from 29 % to 1 %). It renders again before the canvas scrolls back in, and a jump straight into view is rendered in the frame that shows it. The frame clock keeps running, so an effect resumes at the state it has at that time. A `trigger()` or `settled()` that is pending while the canvas is out of view still resolves: the host renders about 10 frames per second until it settles. `host.stats()` gains `paused`, `pauses` and `offscreenFrames`. No option: the pause cannot be seen.
- Snapshot builds read less of the live page, with unchanged output: the clone walk measures the drawable once per build instead of once per list row, reads scroll metrics only of scroll containers and form controls, and no longer creates an `Attr` node per attribute or copies child lists. A parity test builds the examples, the test fixtures and edge cases (transforms, sticky, scrollers, rounded boxes, long lists, shadow DOM, forms, selections) with the walk of 0.1.0-preview.1 and the new one and requires identical snapshots. Per build (p50 / p95 ms, headed Chrome 155, DPR 2): an inner-scroll list of 300 rows 2.11 / 2.54 -> 1.52 / 2.06, the F2 list 4.58 / 6.47 -> 2.89 / 3.68, a 60 Hz counter 1.34 / 1.48 -> 1.05 / 1.22, F4 with 200 elements 1.78 / 3.17 -> 1.31 / 2.25, F4 with 2000 elements 8.53 / 10.28 -> 5.38 / 6.36, typing in a textarea 1.53 / 2.52 -> 1.45 / 2.27.

### Fixed

- A damage-pruned tile could move every grid item after an element left out of it: the stand-in of that element lost its `id`, and with it an id-based placement (`#bar { grid-column: 1 / -1 }`), so the list column of F2 showed the side panel after a `:hover` change of one row. Stand-ins keep their `id` now.
- The scroll compositor's classic-scrollbar thumb tile was empty (its capture hid the whole SVG image), so the thumb went missing from composed frames.
- A large scroller's pages were dropped because the capture reported the node budget (a limited entry).

## [0.1.0-preview.1] - 2026-10-17

First preview, published as `@giuseppe-scotto-lavina/html-in-canvas-polyfill` under the npm dist-tag `next`.

### Added

- The proposed HTML-in-Canvas API over a live DOM: `content="drawable"` on the canvas and the `drawable` attribute on drawn elements (legacy `layoutsubtree` / `layoutSubtree` accepted with one deprecation warning); the `paint` event with `changedElements`, `onpaint` and `requestPaint()`; `drawElementImage` (all overloads) on 2D and main-thread `OffscreenCanvas` contexts; `captureElementImage` and `ElementImage` (`close()`); `getElementImageDefaultSize` on the canvas and on its main-thread `OffscreenCanvas`; `updateElementGeometry`, `clearElementGeometry`, `getElementTransform` and `elementgeometryupdate`. The API follows the HTML Standard pull request as of 2026-10-08.
- WebGL and WebGL2 `texElementSubImage2D` (and the legacy `texElementImage2D`), WebGPU `GPUQueue.drawElementImageToTexture` (and the legacy `copyElementImageToTexture`), in the opt-in `hic-live-gpu` bundle.
- Native-first install: a behavioural probe of the core (`content="drawable"`, the `paint` handler, a 2D `drawElementImage` that behaves like a platform method) chooses between running the polyfill and stepping aside for the browser's own implementation. A usable native implementation is never refused, whatever secondary members it lacks, renames or adds; `installed.report()` records what differs (`native-step-aside`). The optional `hic-live-native-bridge` adds what it can build over the earlier `layoutsubtree` draft. Only a native implementation without its core is refused (`HicUnsupportedError('partial-native')`).
- Spec alignment notes: `ElementImage` has `close()` only, its size is `canvas.getElementImageDefaultSize(elementImage)` (whatwg/html#11588, 2026-10-08), with no `width` / `height` alias because native Chrome has none. The two-argument `getElementTransform(element, drawTransform)` and `OffscreenCanvas.getElementTransform` remain as deprecated legacy overloads until the pinned WPT revision moves. `options.mode: 'polyfill'` over a usable native implementation steps aside with one warning.
- Desktop Chrome, Firefox 155+ and Safari 27+. On Firefox and Safari the opt-in `hic-live-engines` bundle draws form controls, classic scrollbars and shadows in each engine's look.
- Typed text in single-line fields is drawn at once, inner scrolling is composed from cached pages, and snapshot builds run after the frame, so typing and wheel scrolling are close to native latency (`docs/PERFORMANCE-COMPARISON.md`).
- Extensions of this library, not part of the standard and not provided by native implementations: a frame cap (`framecap` attribute, `canvas.frameCap`) and a WebGPU effect-plugin host with input remapping (`HTMLInCanvasEffects`, plugin ABI v2). They work only while the polyfill provides the API (mode `polyfill`).
- Eight example effect plugins outside the polyfill, in `plugins/` (one folder each, found by the build by convention): `crt`, `disintegrate`, `glitch`, `holo-card`, `liquid-glass`, `page-curl`, `shatter` and `water`. Bundles `dist/plugins/<name>.iife(.min).js` and `.esm.js`, package subpaths `./plugins/<name>`, a manifest (with each plugin's reduced-motion behaviour), a plugin template, `plugins/_lib/` for runtime code that several plugins share, and a test kit that checks a plugin's input mapper against the GPU pixels and drives it in the gallery with trusted input (`npm run test:plugins`).
- Plugin actions, a uniform way to run what a plugin can do: a definition may declare `actions` (ABI v2), and the effect handle of `host.addPlugin` gains `actions`, `trigger(action, options?)` and `settled()`. `trigger` resolves with the state the plugin rests in once the frame that shows it was rendered, or with `null` when a newer action superseded it, the plugin was removed, disabled or failed, or the GPU device was lost; an unknown action, a plugin without actions or bad options reject with a `TypeError` that names what is accepted. The host reads `prefers-reduced-motion` at each trigger and passes it to the plugin; the plugin's own submit handlers and buttons take the same path. Actions of the example plugins: disintegrate `out` / `in` / `play`, shatter `impact` / `crack` / `out` / `in` / `play` (with an optional impact `point`), glitch `burst`, page-curl `peek`, water `drop`; the template shows one (`shift`). The gallery shows a button per action. Reference: `docs/guides/EFFECTS-PLUGINS.md` section 5, `docs/API-CONTRACT.md` 7.3.1.
- `examples/effect-plugin`: the smallest page with effects (about 30 lines of script): the host, the water plugin, the frame cap and a disintegrate transition run with `handle.trigger` from the page's submit handler, with the 2D fallback.
- `NO_TARGET` in the effects ABI (`HTMLInCanvasEffects.NO_TARGET`, `isNoTarget`; exported by the effects module): what an input mapper returns where the presented pixel shows no element. The mapper chain stops at it, so an earlier plugin cannot clamp it back onto the canvas, and the interposer swallows that gesture (no focus change, no activation, no click).
- `npm run test:pages`: the GitHub Pages site under its project path in headless Chrome, every example and the gallery with each plugin.
- Classic `<script>` bundles (readable and minified) and an ES module build with `exports` and TypeScript declarations; no runtime dependencies and no network request on load.
- Six examples, among them a gallery of the effect plugins over one live form and the smallest page with effects, and a zero-dependency demo server (`npm run demo`); guides for getting started, frameworks (React, Vue, Svelte, Lit), effect plugins (using and writing them), performance and troubleshooting.

Android WebView, in-app iOS web views and Firefox for Android stay refused, with the reason named. Phones get their own surface budgets (`maxDpr` 3, 4096 x 4096 px per surface, 256 MiB in all, 128 MiB on devices reporting 2 GB or less), and iOS form controls are drawn in the iOS look (system blue tint, pill buttons and pop-up buttons, the colour-well hue ring, medium-style date and time pills, no number spinner). Guide: `docs/guides/MOBILE.md`.
- `npm run test:mobile`: Mobile Safari in the iOS Simulator (`safaridriver`) and Chrome in the Android Emulator (adb and the DevTools protocol): taps on moved drawables, typing, touch scroll, the virtual keyboard, rotation, long-press selection, the iOS control look (62 regions) and the refusal of older browsers, each with a positive control.

With its HTML-in-Canvas internal feature off it runs the polyfill scenarios and reports the native-flag scenarios skipped; with it on it runs them: install steps aside, the explainer and the contact form drawn natively through the bridge (pixels against the plain-DOM twin, trusted clicks, typing and repaint), uninstall identity and the examples, each with a positive control.
