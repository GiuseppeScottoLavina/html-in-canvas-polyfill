## [1.0.0-rc.8] - 2026-11-10

The eighth release candidate of 1.0.0, no API change. An external audit of `1.0.0-rc.7` found a regression of that release: a canvas inserted again under another parent after a suspension stopped following the attributes of its new ancestors. The ancestor-move matrix built for it (69 cases, in Chrome, Firefox and Safari) found four older defects of the same family, fixed here too. A check of the whole project on Safari, on an iPad and on Firefox then found that Safari on macOS had composed no inner scrolling since 0.1.0-preview.23, fixed here as well.

- The core bundle is 374,275 bytes minified, 127,677 gzipped: 7,664 and 2,890 bytes more than 1.0.0-rc.7 (6,288 and 2,319 of them the two changes to pruned snapshots below).

### Fixed

- A canvas inserted again under another parent after it was suspended (removed for a moment, then put back elsewhere before it was dropped) kept watching the attributes of its former ancestors: a class or an inherited custom property set on its new parent or higher did not repaint it, while a change on the former parent did. 1.0.0-rc.7 brought this in, when the damage of a canvas active again stopped being `forced` for the runaway guard; the watcher of the ancestors re-read them on `forced` damage only. The watcher now re-reads them after every insertion or removal in an observed scope, whatever the cause of the damage (external audit of 1.0.0-rc.7).
- The same fix covers moves that no release followed: a canvas moved to another parent inside one task, an ancestor of a canvas moved, a canvas in a shadow tree moved there, and a move under a parent that already sets an inherited custom property, which kept the old value on the canvas.
- A canvas in a shadow tree whose host was removed and inserted in another place across a checkpoint stayed suspended, its content out of the document, until the grace period dropped it: the registry now reconciles a suspended canvas that is connected again after any insertion.
- The clone of a canvas in a shadow tree now has the language the browser gives the canvas: the language set above its host in Chrome, only that of `<html>` in Safari, none in Firefox (measured; before, `<html lang>` everywhere and nothing set in between), so `:lang()` rules and the font fallback of the language match the page.
- Safari on macOS composed no inner scrolling since 0.1.0-preview.23: every plan of the scroll compositor was refused (`refusedBy.cover` in the report's scroll stats) and every step was rebuilt. The check added in that release reads the port without the scroller's content and wants every pixel in the cover colour, and Safari on macOS does not draw the four corner pixels of an image exactly (27.0.1: up to 4 levels off, an alpha of 253 to 255, different from one drawing to the next, whatever the image; Safari on an iPad draws them exactly, so the mobile gate passed). Measured on a 300-row list at 60 Hz: 44 to 53 canvas updates a second and a frame of 133 to 146 ms at each attempt, against 59 and at most 25 ms on the commit before the check. On WebKit the check now leaves out the corner pixels of its tiles, and no other pixel. A second cause refused every scroller with the system's classic bars (macOS shows them while a mouse is attached): that bar is painted as a background layer of the list, after the clone set the first cover colour, and it declared the list's own colour again, so the first cover tile showed the page through the list. The cover colours are now set on the finished clone, the first one too. After both, the same list: 58 updates a second, 172 to 174 of about 175 steps composed, the longest frame 42 to 55 ms.
- The cover check costs less where the canvas is not Chromium's. On WebKit its tiles are rendered with one pixel per css px (the image is laid out in css px and scaled, so the tile holds the same boxes): a quarter of the pixels at a device pixel ratio of 2. Where a read of the canvas costs by the call (Safari on macOS: 40 to 50 ms for every `getImageData` that follows a drawing, whatever its size, so about 360 ms for the nine bands of one plan), the rest of a tile is read in one task instead of band by band (a tile that fits one band, as most do at one pixel per css px, is read once either way). On an iPad (Safari 27.2, 120 Hz, a 300-row list scrolled at once after its first paint) the first composed step comes 71 to 104 ms after the first scroll, from 99 to 123 ms (46 to 59 ms before the check existed); in Safari on macOS it comes after 114 to 115 ms (66 to 68 ms before the check; with the corner pixels alone left out and the bands kept, 459 to 488 ms).
- A snapshot of a large drawable lost the margins that had collapsed through a part left out of it. Out of the changed part a group of rows is replaced by an empty stand-in, and the top margin of its first row (or the bottom margin of its last), which had collapsed through the group, went with the rows: everything after the group stood that margin too high in the tile (a 10 px margin: the tile 10 px off, 42 % of its pixels). It showed where stand-ins are not merged with their neighbours: children of different types, list items, or, up to this release, any page with a selector that counts siblings. The stand-in now takes the gap the live boxes have.
- Safari and Firefox: a form control that the page sizes with a percentage (`width: 100%`, a `calc()` with one) could move its neighbours in the drawn picture. The engines bundle draws the controls in these browsers and pins each one's box, and it wrote the width in px; a px width gives the same box, but it counts in full for the intrinsic sizes of the container, where a percentage counts as `auto`. In a grid of `1fr auto` with a full-width range across both columns and a readout with a `min-width` in the second one, the range's px took the whole row for the first column, the second stayed at the readout's min-width, and its text was drawn out of the row: 52 px to the right, cut at the canvas edge, in the frame cap's readout of the plugin gallery on an iPad (the gallery's own row in Firefox was not affected; the fixture's rows are). The percentage is now kept, with the page's own min-width and max-width. In Safari it is read from the computed style; Firefox has no CSS Typed OM (157), so there it is the width the page declares, where an inline style sets it or the rules that match the control agree on one (rules that differ, a width from a custom property or a sheet the page cannot read keep the px, as before). The controls fixture has two such rows (64 regions): on the code before, both are off on the iPad and in Firefox; now they match the plain page (measured on a physical iPad, Safari 27.2, and in Firefox 157: no other region changed). Not run in Safari on macOS. The engines bundle is 46,325 bytes minified, 17,697 gzipped (544 and 288 more).

### Added

- Plugin `ink` (experimental, like every example plugin): the page as wet ink on a thin layer of water. The pointer stirs an incompressible 2D fluid simulated on the CPU (vorticity and stream function, a multigrid solve, 120 steps a second, the same motion at 30, 60 and 120 fps) that smears, swirls and stretches the live page, which then heals; a press or a finger also releases two inks that the flow folds into each other, shaded as an absorbing and scattering layer (it darkens a bright page and shows on a dark one). The input mapper reads the same displacement field with the shader's filter: 0 pixels wrong of 150,178 against the GPU. At rest the frame is the page, bit for bit, and nothing renders. Measured on an Apple M1 Max at 1280 x 800 and a device pixel ratio of 2: 0.70 ms of CPU and 1.01 ms of GPU per frame at 120 fps while it plays. 39,174 bytes minified. `plugins/ink/README.md`.
- Plugin `cloth` (experimental): the page printed on a sheet of fabric that hangs from its top edge (or its two top corners), takes a push from the pointer, a pull or a gust, falls when its pins let go, and settles flat. A particle grid on the CPU (each thread of the weave solved as a whole, 480 steps a second), drawn as a bicubic B-spline surface with 4x MSAA, a fabric sheen, curvature occlusion, a shadow map for its own folds and for the wall behind, and a linen back. The input mapper casts the line of sight against the triangles the GPU drew: 0 pixels wrong of 150,930 on a pulled cloth. At rest the frame is the page in every pixel, and nothing renders. Measured on an Apple M1 Max: 0.37 ms of GPU per frame at 2560 x 1600 in a gust; 0.60 ms of CPU per frame at 120 fps (measured in Node over a fake device). 46,720 bytes minified. `plugins/cloth/README.md`.
- Effects (experimental): `context.localPoint(point)` in the plugin context. A plugin that reacts to the pointer (a drop under it, a lens that follows it, a card that tilts towards it) gets the pointer's place in the picture it rendered: the point on the canvas through the input mappers of the plugins added after it, in the frame on screen; `null` where a later plugin shows nothing of its picture, and the point itself for the last plugin. Before, the first of two stacked plugins read the pointer on the canvas, so its reaction was seen away from the pointer by what the second one moved (24 css px for a drop of water near the edge of a curved CRT screen, measured; 4 now). The water, liquid-glass, holo-card, page-curl and shatter plugins read the pointer through it (`plugins/_lib/pointer.js`). docs/guides/EFFECTS-PLUGINS.md section 12, docs/API-CONTRACT.md 7.2.
- Effects (experimental): `host.mapPoint(point)`. Where a point of the canvas is in the source: the point through every plugin's input mapper in the frame on screen, or `null` where the plugins show nothing of the source. It is what the host does with a pointer, for a page that wants to show or log what lies under the pointer as the user sees it. With `localPoint`, the effects bundle is 40,999 bytes minified, 14,486 gzipped (847 and 304 more); the core is unchanged.

### Changed

- Large drawables on pages whose CSS counts siblings update faster. A snapshot of a drawable above 350 elements leaves out what lies beyond the changed part: rows become empty stand-ins, runs of them are merged, the rest under the change is left out. One selector that counts siblings (`:nth-child()`, `:first-child`, `:last-child`, `+`, `~`...) anywhere in the page's CSS, whatever it selected, used to turn the merging and the cut off for every drawable, and almost every stylesheet has one. Each such selector is now tied to the lists it can match, and `:first-child`, `:last-child`, `:only-child` and `:empty` no longer cost anything. Measured, a counter updated in every frame beside 1,000 cells with a `.x:nth-child(even)` rule that matches nothing there: 59 canvas updates a second on an iPad (Safari 27.2) from 30, 60 in Chrome from 41, 59 in Firefox from 39; beside 300 rows with `.row:last-child` and `.row + .row` rules: 59.5 on the iPad from 52. Rows that a `:nth-child()` rule really stripes keep one stand-in each, as before. `docs/guides/PERFORMANCE.md` section 3 says what each kind of selector costs.
- Live demo: the plugin gallery has three scenes for the same effects, chosen with a switch drawn in the canvas (`examples/plugins/`): the form of before, a daylight page (light colours and a landscape made of CSS gradients) and live data (a clock, bars and a feed that change every second). The scenes are plain HTML of the same canvas, so every plugin runs on each of them unchanged; `?scene=daylight` or `?scene=live` opens one, and the choice stays in the address. With live data the effect draws only when the page changed (3 to 120 frames in 3.2 s in the gate, none in 2 s on the form). On a phone the scene's picture sits beside its title, and the stage is at least 640 px tall (580 before), so that the form fits it without an inner scroll with every effect's controls, also at the height a phone browser leaves under its bars (measured at 360 x 640, 375 x 667, 390 x 667, 390 x 844 and 430 x 932). G-gallery checks the scenes on a phone and on a tablet layout with a touch on the drawn switch, and has a third view, a short phone (375 x 667), where it checks that the form fits for every plugin and scene: 224 checks and 12 controls in all, 4 of the controls new (docs/TEST-PLAN.md).
- Live demo: the plugin gallery can run a second effect over the first. A smaller strip of chips under the effects ("+ on top") adds one more plugin to the same host: it reads the picture the first one rendered, a click is mapped back through both to the field you see, and the first effect reads the pointer through the second. With two effects the form has a switch between their controls, and the panel describes both; `&then=<name>` selects the second effect on load. Every ordered pair of the plugins runs (90 for the ten of this release, checked by the gate). On a phone the hero shows its title alone (the eyebrow and the lede are gone there): under the bar and the two strips the stage has no room for more with every effect's controls. G-gallery has a part for it (pick, the switch, rest, the pointer through a curved screen, every pair) with four controls (docs/TEST-PLAN.md).
- Live demo: the plugin gallery has an input map (the button in the bar, `&map=1`). It draws over the stage what the host does with the pointer: the pointer, the place in the page it is sent to through the effect, the frame of the real element there (in its true position, under the canvas) and a label with the element and what it holds now. Under the CRT monitor at full curvature the two points are 39.5 css px apart at the email field (measured by the gate, which taps there and finds the field named, framed and focused; one control).
- Tests: G-prune-structure (26 rules one by one and 144 random lists and selector sets: every element of a pruned clone matches exactly the selectors its live element matches; 14 mutants) and G-prune-margins (8 cases, 5 mutants), docs/TEST-PLAN.md 2.4. The random part found the margin defect above.
- Tests: G-xbrowser `scrollCompose` on Safari has a mutant that reads the corner pixels again (nothing composes: the state of 0.1.0-preview.23 to 1.0.0-rc.7 on a Mac), one that leaves the first cover colour where the clone declared it (the lists with the system's bars are refused) and an overlay case (a line one css px thin across both lists refuses their plans, read from tiles of one pixel per css px; its control is a check that passes whatever it reads). `test/unit/snapshot-store/scroll-vet-tiles.browser.test.mjs` covers the corner pixels, the tiles on the grid and the slow read, each with its control. This gate runs on a Mac only (the hosted runners have Safari 26): it was red from 0.1.0-preview.23 on and was not run.
- Tests: `npm test` now checks the anchors of the mutant bundles that the controls of G-xbrowser and G-mobile `scrollCompose` serve (12, with the 94 of the gates). Those gates run on a Mac and on devices only, and the `corner` mutant of G-mobile had not matched since 0.1.0-preview.23 (the bundler had numbered the constant it named), so that control could not run; it names the expression now.
- Tests: the ancestor-move matrix (`test/unit/schedule/ancestor-move-*.browser.test.mjs`, docs/TEST-PLAN.md G-ancestor-move, 69 cases), in Chrome, in Firefox in the macOS CI and in Safari locally (`test/xbrowser/ancestor-move.mjs`); on 1.0.0-rc.7 the same cases fail 32 of 69 in Chrome. The unit host page puts the message of a failed assertion first in Firefox and Safari, whose stacks leave it out.
- Docs: docs/BUNDLES.md states the new ceilings (core 374.5 KB raw and 127.75 KB gzipped, engines bundle 46.375 and 17.75 KB, module loader 139.25 and 156.25 KB); docs/ARCHITECTURE.md describes the cover check's tiles on WebKit, the selectors that count siblings and the margins of pruned children; docs/ARCHITECTURE.md describes the tree-change signal in the lifecycle; version 1.0.0-rc.8.

## [1.0.0-rc.7] - 2026-11-09

The seventh release candidate of 1.0.0, no API change. An external audit of `1.0.0-rc.6` found no functional defect; its requests for precision gave each part of the listener matrix its own positive control, and those controls found a defect of the runaway guard that was there before (Fixed, below).

- The core bundle is 366,611 bytes minified, 124,787 gzipped: 34 and 14 bytes more than 1.0.0-rc.6.

### Fixed

- Runaway guard: a page that toggled a canvas between drawable and fallback content many times a second, with events reaching its listeners in between, could make the guard take the remounts for a feedback loop and uninstall the polyfill ("committed 90 times in 3000 ms with nothing on the page asking for it"). The damage of a mount, and of a canvas active again, was `forced`, which the guard counts as the pipeline's own; it is now `content-attr`, damage from outside the pipeline as API-CONTRACT 9.1 lists it, and rendered alike (`src/schedule/scheduler.js`). The damage of a tab shown again stays `forced`. Found by the per-part positive controls of the listener matrix below, whose second round of remounts tripped it every time; `test/unit/api-core/runaway-remount.browser.test.mjs` replays that sequence and fails on the code before.

### Changed

- Tests: each part of the listener matrix has its own positive control (the same cases with a canvas that drops `once` must diverge) and a fixed number of cases, in Chrome, in Firefox and Safari (`test/xbrowser/listener-matrix.mjs`) and on a device; `this` is checked exactly (the object for a `handleEvent` listener, the canvas for a function) where either was accepted. From an external audit of 1.0.0-rc.6.

## [1.0.0-rc.6] - 2026-11-09

The sixth release candidate of 1.0.0, no API change. The finding of an external audit of `1.0.0-rc.5` was the fifth in a row about the forwarding of canvas listeners, each found one combination at a time. So this release adds the listener matrix, a test that compares a canvas listener with the same listener on a native element over about 20,000 combinations in Chrome, Firefox and Safari, and fixes everything it found: the audit's case and two classes that no audit had found (Fixed, below). Run on the bridges of 1.0.0-rc.1 to rc.5, the matrix finds each audit's finding.

- The core bundle is 366,577 bytes minified, 124,773 gzipped: 779 and 238 bytes more than 1.0.0-rc.5 (about 250 of them are the restoration rule in the `tree-identity` row, which the classic bundle carries).

### Added

- Tests: the listener matrix (`test/unit/live/listener-matrix.mjs`, docs/TEST-PLAN.md 2.3). It covers:
  - every combination of a canvas listener's options;
  - what its callback does (stopping, cancelling, a nested dispatch by either route, removing itself, adding another);
  - two listeners and their order, including cancelling through native methods saved before the polyfill;
  - an `on*` handler next to a listener;
  - the lifecycle: removal, abort, remount, unmounted window, uninstall and install, uninstalled window;
  - both routes in both orders.

  Every case runs on the polyfill's canvas and on a native element with a child, and the two are compared call by call. It runs in Chrome in the browser unit tests, in Firefox in the macOS CI (`test/xbrowser/listener-matrix.mjs`) and in Safari locally and on the iPad (`test/unit/live/listener-matrix.html`), with a positive control. 0 divergences in all three engines.

### Changed

- Docs: the test plan records the VoiceOver session on this candidate (2026-11-09, Safari 27.0.1 on macOS 27.0.1, all 13 steps as on the reference).

### Fixed

- Events: after an uninstall and an install, a `once` canvas listener could run twice if it stopped the event's propagation or dispatched an event at a child (the audit's case). A reinstall now registers the bridge's listeners again natively, in their order, each `once` listener behind a new guard, so a guard always runs before its listener.
- Events: an `on*` handler property of the canvas (`canvas.onclick = fn`) returning `false` did not cancel an event of a relocated child, and on the children's route it did not keep its place among the canvas listeners (it was registered once per mount and ran after them). Handlers are now entries of the same ordered registry: a handler sits where it was first set, keeps its place when it is replaced and goes last when it is set to null and set again, as natively; it is read from the canvas at each event and `false` cancels.
- Events: while the canvas was unmounted (`content` set to `fallback`), a listener removed from the canvas was still called through the layer after the next mount, and one added then never got its children's events after it. The bridge's methods now last while the canvas is managed, mounted or not; only the forwarders are per mount.
- Contract: the `tree-identity` row states what a reinstall restores. The polyfill sees no change to the canvas listeners while it is not installed, so they are restored as they were at the uninstall: one removed or run (`once`) meanwhile is registered again, and one added meanwhile gets only the events aimed at the canvas. Handlers are read from the canvas. This replaces the sentence of 1.0.0-rc.5, which covered only part of it.

The fifth release candidate of 1.0.0: the fix for an external audit of `1.0.0-rc.4`, no API change. A `once` listener on the canvas now runs once, as natively, whichever route its event takes (Fixed, below).

### Changed

- Docs: the test plan records the VoiceOver session on this candidate (2026-11-09, Safari 27.0.1 on macOS 27.0.1, all 13 steps as on the reference).

### Fixed

- Events: a `once` listener on the canvas could run twice, once for an event of a relocated child and once for an event aimed at the canvas itself, in either order. The polyfill registers it natively on the canvas and as a forwarder on the layer, and each consumed it on its own. Now a guard, a passive native listener of the same type, phase and signal set on the canvas before the listener's own registration, ends the registration when an event reaches the canvas, and the forwarder ends it and removes the native registration and the guard before it calls the listener. The `tree-identity` row states the one case left: a once listener still pending at an uninstall that fires before a reinstall can run once more after it. Present before 1.0.0-rc.3; reported by an external audit of 1.0.0-rc.4, reproduced on Chrome and on Safari on an iPad.
- Tests: G-resize-drag tells a slow machine from a slow polyfill. A drag that still showed the page fewer than 8 sizes after its attempts is made once more on the plain-DOM twin: when the twin sees too few sizes as well, the run is NOT USABLE, with the reason (gates can now say so, `ctx.unusable`); when the twin sees enough, the check fails as before.
- Changelog: the 1.0.0-rc.4 entry says precisely which removal failed (one after an abort, not one alone).

## [1.0.0-rc.4] - 2026-11-08

The fourth release candidate of 1.0.0: the fixes for an external audit of `1.0.0-rc.3`, no API change. It fixes a regression of `1.0.0-rc.3` in the forwarding of canvas listeners (Fixed, below).

- The core bundle is 365,353 bytes minified, 124,388 gzipped: 42 and 6 bytes more than 1.0.0-rc.3.

### Changed

- Docs: the test plan records the VoiceOver session on this candidate (2026-11-08, Safari 27.0.1 on macOS 27.0.1, all 13 steps as on the reference).

### Fixed

- Events (a regression of 1.0.0-rc.3): after the canvas was unmounted and mounted again (its `content` toggled, for example), aborting the `signal` of a canvas listener left its forwarder on the layer, so the listener kept being called, and a later `removeEventListener` did not remove it either (a removal alone after a remount was correct); registering the same function again then called it twice per event. Reported by an external audit of 1.0.0-rc.3, reproduced on Chrome and on Safari on an iPad.
- Mutant tests: a gate process that printed a PASS result line and then exited non-zero or was killed is not usable, so it can no longer stand as a clean baseline with no red check; an ordinary FAIL (a red check, exit 1) is unchanged.

The mutant runner tells a flaky check by its name, so a value that changed between runs hid one; the values are now in the detail, and the checks and thresholds are unchanged.

## [1.0.0-rc.3] - 2026-11-07

The third release candidate of 1.0.0: the fixes for an external audit of `1.0.0-rc.2`, no API change. One stable row, the forwarding of canvas listeners, now leaves the passive rules to the browser (Fixed, below).

- The core bundle is 365,311 bytes minified, 124,382 gzipped: 1,066 and 314 bytes less than 1.0.0-rc.2 (the listener fix below replaces code).

### Fixed

- Events: a passive canvas listener could still cancel the event through a reference to the native `Event.prototype.preventDefault`, or to the `returnValue` setter, saved before the polyfill was installed (a library loaded first keeps one), when a non-passive listener of the same type and phase was on the canvas. The fix of 1.0.0-rc.2 patched the prototype during the call, which such a reference bypasses. - Mutant tests: a mutant could be reported CAUGHT against a clean run that did not run or was not usable; it is now NOT USABLE. A mutant whose only new red checks were also red on one of the two clean runs (a flaky check) is UNCERTAIN, which fails the suite, instead of CAUGHT. The decisions are in `test/gates/mutants/verdict.mjs`, with node tests, and the receipt keeps both clean runs.
- Docs: `SECURITY.md` says what the loader downloads in each case, as `docs/STABILITY.md` does.

- Docs: the test plan records the VoiceOver session on this candidate (2026-11-07, Safari 27.0.1 on macOS 27.0.1, all 13 steps as on the reference).
- Mutant tests: a mutant that any of its gates may catch is CAUGHT by one usable gate that caught it, even when another of its gates was dropped (`paint-nothing`, caught by G-f1-form while its G-explainer run was dropped because the OS pointer moved, was reported NOT USABLE); a mutant that every gate must catch still needs every gate usable.

- A field test page on the live demo ([examples/field-test/](examples/field-test/), [docs/guides/FIELD-TEST.md](docs/guides/FIELD-TEST.md)): a guided five-minute check that a person without technical knowledge runs on a machine we do not have. It records the environment (browser and platform versions, pixel ratio, colour settings, system colours, scrollbars, fonts, GPU adapter, the polyfill's install result and report), compares the canvas with the same HTML as a plain page through a capture of the tab where the browser allows it (Chrome and Edge on desktop), measures typing latency, inner-scroll update rates, the water effect and a 125 % zoom, and downloads one JSON report with small PNG crops. `npm run test:field` runs it end to end in headless Chrome with a deliberately broken canvas as the positive control; `scripts/field-report.mjs` prints a report and extracts its crops; a "Field test report" issue template takes the file.

### Fixed

- Scrollbars, layout: an unsized `<textarea>` or listbox `<select>` was drawn about 15 css px narrower than it is laid out (Chrome on Linux and Windows, on macOS with classic bars and with overlay bars too, and with author-styled `::-webkit-scrollbar` bars), because Chrome counts the bar thickness in their intrinsic size whether or not a bar shows and the snapshot has no bars; the controls after it reflowed in the canvas, away from where they are hit (29-32 of the 62 regions of the platform probe's form-control page failed with classic bars, 16 with macOS overlay bars). The snapshot now keeps their live size. A box that reserves a bar gutter without scrolling (`overflow: scroll`, a listbox, `scrollbar-gutter: stable`) keeps its gutter too.
- Classic scrollbars, look: Chrome's bars are drawn as Chrome draws them on the platform: the Fluent bars of Linux and Windows (arrow buttons, the thumb between them, the light and dark colours, Windows' arrow glyphs where its icon font is installed) and the macOS bars (inset thumb, track borders, the dark look), with the engine's thumb length and position rules at every device pixel ratio, `scrollbar-width: thin` and `scrollbar-color`; listbox `<select>` bars are drawn. Which look is measured from the bar thicknesses, not read from the user agent. Platform probe: every bar region of F6 and F7 within 0.7 % of native at DPR 1, 1.25 and 1.5 on Linux and macOS (was 12-100 %).
- Text drawn a little blurred at a fractional device pixel ratio when the canvas content box is not a whole number of device pixels (a 1 px border is 0.8 css px at DPR 1.25): the ResizeObserver device-pixel pattern rounds each axis on its own, the snapshot was rasterised at the grid's scale and a default-size draw stretched it by a hair to the other axis. Such a canvas is now rasterised at the device pixel ratio, as native draws it, and drawn 1:1. The field test's scroll box at DPR 1.25 (3.6-8 % of its pixels over 16) now matches the plain page.

### Known limitations

- Measured on Windows by the platform probe (headless Chrome 156 on the CI runner): a text selection without an author `::selection` rule is painted by Chrome in its default rgba(0, 65, 198, 0.8), while the polyfill draws the system colour Highlight (#0078d4 there), so the drawn selection is a different blue; the selected glyphs (HighlightText) and author rules match. A headed Windows Chrome was not measured. Row `caret-selection`.

## [0.1.0-preview.2] - 2026-10-19

A performance release. Scrolling inside a drawable is presented at the display rate in Chrome, effects do no work while their canvas cannot be seen, and snapshot builds read less of the live page. The API is unchanged; `host.stats()` of the effect host gains three fields.

### Performance

- Scrolling inside a drawable is presented at the display rate in Chrome. A live scroller under the pointer (or one that scrolls) is given an invisible main-thread repaint reason, so Chrome's compositor waits for the frame that redraws the canvas instead of delivering the scroll offset in every second frame. Measured at 120 Hz with a trusted wheel stream: a 300-row list from 61 to 113 canvas updates per second (native: 116), with a `:hover` style on its rows from 61 to 61-109 (those steps are built, each hovered row being a change, as often as the main-thread budget allows), rounded corners from 60 to 104, 2000 rows from 4 to 95, the F2 fixture from 24 to 81; main-thread time of the plain list from 36 % to 24 % of the stream. The scroller's look is untouched: its attribute and rule never reach a snapshot, an author transition on `box-shadow` sees no transition from it, and a scroller whose shadow or background is animated, a shadow host and a slotted scroller are left alone (`docs/API-CONTRACT.md` 4.9). Firefox and Safari are not changed by it.
- The scroll compositor plans a scroller when the pointer reaches it (or at its first scroll event), so the first gesture is composed; it keeps its cached pages when something elsewhere in the drawable changes (a counter beside the list), marks the part of the pages a change inside the list touches as stale until the next tile repairs it, composes rounded scrollports (also with a classic scrollbar) inside their rounded clip, and plans scrollers of up to 20,000 elements in steps. Its page builds measure the truncation of a long list once per scroller instead of once per page (2000 rows: page builds p50 6.5 to 5.2 ms).
- `options.maxSnapshotFps` and the `nodeBudget.hard` interval no longer hold the build of a scroller's own scroll damage (rows hovered by a still pointer may ride along); the framecap and the main-thread budget still do. Every other change keeps both limits (`docs/API-CONTRACT.md` 7.1). A 2000-row list now follows every wheel step in Firefox and Safari too (before: 3 to 5 updates per stream of about 60 steps).
- `:hover` rules that only change paint (colours, backgrounds, shadows, outlines, opacity) damage the hovered element alone, also the first time it is hovered.
- The effect host does no work for a canvas nobody can see: while the canvas is more than half a viewport (or half its scroll container) out of view, or the document is hidden, it renders and uploads nothing, `continuous` plugins included (gallery with water: renderer + GPU process CPU from 29 % to 1 %). It renders again before the canvas scrolls back in, and a jump straight into view is rendered in the frame that shows it. The frame clock keeps running, so an effect resumes at the state it has at that time. A `trigger()` or `settled()` that is pending while the canvas is out of view still resolves: the host renders about 10 frames per second until it settles. `host.stats()` gains `paused`, `pauses` and `offscreenFrames`. No option: the pause cannot be seen.
- Snapshot builds read less of the live page, with unchanged output: the clone walk measures the drawable once per build instead of once per list row, reads scroll metrics only of scroll containers and form controls, and no longer creates an `Attr` node per attribute or copies child lists. A parity test builds the examples, the test fixtures and edge cases (transforms, sticky, scrollers, rounded boxes, long lists, shadow DOM, forms, selections) with the walk of 0.1.0-preview.1 and the new one and requires identical snapshots. Per build (p50 / p95 ms, headed Chrome 155, DPR 2): an inner-scroll list of 300 rows 2.11 / 2.54 -> 1.52 / 2.06, the F2 list 4.58 / 6.47 -> 2.89 / 3.68, a 60 Hz counter 1.34 / 1.48 -> 1.05 / 1.22, F4 with 200 elements 1.78 / 3.17 -> 1.31 / 2.25, F4 with 2000 elements 8.53 / 10.28 -> 5.38 / 6.36, typing in a textarea 1.53 / 2.52 -> 1.45 / 2.27.

### Fixed

- A damage-pruned tile could move every grid item after an element left out of it: the stand-in of that element lost its `id`, and with it an id-based placement (`#bar { grid-column: 1 / -1 }`), so the list column of F2 showed the side panel after a `:hover` change of one row. Stand-ins keep their `id` now.
- The scroll compositor's classic-scrollbar thumb tile was empty (its capture hid the whole SVG image), so the thumb went missing from composed frames.
- A large scroller's pages were dropped because the capture reported the node budget (a limited entry).

## [0.1.0-preview.1] - 2026-10-17

First preview, published as `@giuseppe-scotto-lavina/html-in-canvas-polyfill` under the npm dist-tag `next`.

### Added

- The proposed HTML-in-Canvas API over a live DOM: `content="drawable"` on the canvas and the `drawable` attribute on drawn elements (legacy `layoutsubtree` / `layoutSubtree` accepted with one deprecation warning); the `paint` event with `changedElements`, `onpaint` and `requestPaint()`; `drawElementImage` (all overloads) on 2D and main-thread `OffscreenCanvas` contexts; `captureElementImage` and `ElementImage` (`close()`); `getElementImageDefaultSize` on the canvas and on its main-thread `OffscreenCanvas`; `updateElementGeometry`, `clearElementGeometry`, `getElementTransform` and `elementgeometryupdate`. The API follows the HTML Standard pull request as of 2026-10-08.
- WebGL and WebGL2 `texElementSubImage2D` (and the legacy `texElementImage2D`), WebGPU `GPUQueue.drawElementImageToTexture` (and the legacy `copyElementImageToTexture`), in the opt-in `hic-live-gpu` bundle.
- Native-first install: a behavioural probe of the core (`content="drawable"`, the `paint` handler, a 2D `drawElementImage` that behaves like a platform method) chooses between running the polyfill and stepping aside for the browser's own implementation. A usable native implementation is never refused, whatever secondary members it lacks, renames or adds; `installed.report()` records what differs (`native-step-aside`). The optional `hic-live-native-bridge` adds what it can build over the earlier `layoutsubtree` draft. Only a native implementation without its core is refused (`HicUnsupportedError('partial-native')`).
- Spec alignment notes: `ElementImage` has `close()` only, its size is `canvas.getElementImageDefaultSize(elementImage)` (whatwg/html#11588, 2026-10-08), with no `width` / `height` alias because native Chrome has none. The two-argument `getElementTransform(element, drawTransform)` and `OffscreenCanvas.getElementTransform` remain as deprecated legacy overloads until the pinned WPT revision moves. `options.mode: 'polyfill'` over a usable native implementation steps aside with one warning.
- Desktop Chrome, Firefox 155+ and Safari 27+. On Firefox and Safari the opt-in `hic-live-engines` bundle draws form controls, classic scrollbars and shadows in each engine's look.
- Typed text in single-line fields is drawn at once, inner scrolling is composed from cached pages, and snapshot builds run after the frame, so typing and wheel scrolling are close to native latency (`docs/PERFORMANCE-COMPARISON.md`).
- Extensions of this library, not part of the standard and not provided by native implementations: a frame cap (`framecap` attribute, `canvas.frameCap`) and a WebGPU effect-plugin host with input remapping (`HTMLInCanvasEffects`, plugin ABI v2). They work only while the polyfill provides the API (mode `polyfill`).
- Eight example effect plugins outside the polyfill, in `plugins/` (one folder each, found by the build by convention): `crt`, `disintegrate`, `glitch`, `holo-card`, `liquid-glass`, `page-curl`, `shatter` and `water`. Bundles `dist/plugins/<name>.iife(.min).js` and `.esm.js`, package subpaths `./plugins/<name>`, a manifest (with each plugin's reduced-motion behaviour), a plugin template, `plugins/_lib/` for runtime code that several plugins share, and a test kit that checks a plugin's input mapper against the GPU pixels and drives it in the gallery with trusted input (`npm run test:plugins`).
- Plugin actions, a uniform way to run what a plugin can do: a definition may declare `actions` (ABI v2), and the effect handle of `host.addPlugin` gains `actions`, `trigger(action, options?)` and `settled()`. `trigger` resolves with the state the plugin rests in once the frame that shows it was rendered, or with `null` when a newer action superseded it, the plugin was removed, disabled or failed, or the GPU device was lost; an unknown action, a plugin without actions or bad options reject with a `TypeError` that names what is accepted. The host reads `prefers-reduced-motion` at each trigger and passes it to the plugin; the plugin's own submit handlers and buttons take the same path. Actions of the example plugins: disintegrate `out` / `in` / `play`, shatter `impact` / `crack` / `out` / `in` / `play` (with an optional impact `point`), glitch `burst`, page-curl `peek`, water `drop`; the template shows one (`shift`). The gallery shows a button per action. Reference: `docs/guides/EFFECTS-PLUGINS.md` section 5, `docs/API-CONTRACT.md` 7.3.1.
- `examples/effect-plugin`: the smallest page with effects (about 30 lines of script): the host, the water plugin, the frame cap and a disintegrate transition run with `handle.trigger` from the page's submit handler, with the 2D fallback.
- `NO_TARGET` in the effects ABI (`HTMLInCanvasEffects.NO_TARGET`, `isNoTarget`; exported by the effects module): what an input mapper returns where the presented pixel shows no element. The mapper chain stops at it, so an earlier plugin cannot clamp it back onto the canvas, and the interposer swallows that gesture (no focus change, no activation, no click).
- `npm run test:pages`: the GitHub Pages site under its project path in headless Chrome, every example and the gallery with each plugin.
- Classic `<script>` bundles (readable and minified) and an ES module build with `exports` and TypeScript declarations; no runtime dependencies and no network request on load.
- Six examples, among them a gallery of the effect plugins over one live form and the smallest page with effects, and a zero-dependency demo server (`npm run demo`); guides for getting started, frameworks (React, Vue, Svelte, Lit), effect plugins (using and writing them), performance and troubleshooting.
- Phones and tablets, verified on the iOS Simulator and the Android Emulator only (Android: Chromium 158, whose native HTML-in-Canvas makes the polyfill step aside; its own path was run forced): Chrome for Android 130+ and Safari on iOS / iPadOS 26+ are admitted (iPadOS's desktop user agent is recognised by its touch points; Chrome, Firefox and Edge on iOS are WebKit and are admitted by the required features). Android WebView, in-app iOS web views and Firefox for Android stay refused, with the reason named. Phones get their own surface budgets (`maxDpr` 3, 4096 x 4096 px per surface, 256 MiB in all, 128 MiB on devices reporting 2 GB or less), and iOS form controls are drawn in the iOS look (system blue tint, pill buttons and pop-up buttons, the colour-well hue ring, medium-style date and time pills, no number spinner). Guide: `docs/guides/MOBILE.md`.
- `npm run test:mobile`: Mobile Safari in the iOS Simulator (`safaridriver`) and Chrome in the Android Emulator (adb and the DevTools protocol): taps on moved drawables, typing, touch scroll, the virtual keyboard, rotation, long-press selection, the iOS control look (62 regions) and the refusal of older browsers, each with a positive control.
- `report().canvases[].stats.snapshot.rasterScale`: the raster scale of the committed snapshots.
- Safari Technology Preview as an optional engine of `npm run test:xbrowser` (`stp`): skipped with the reason when it is not installed or its remote automation is off; its native surface is read first. With its HTML-in-Canvas internal feature off it runs the polyfill scenarios and reports the native-flag scenarios skipped; with it on it runs them: install steps aside, the explainer and the contact form drawn natively through the bridge (pixels against the plain-DOM twin, trusted clicks, typing and repaint), uninstall identity and the examples, each with a positive control.
- Text selections are drawn in the colours the engine paints them, in single-line fields and in page text (labels, paragraphs, contenteditable): the system Highlight under the glyphs, the glyphs in HighlightText where Chromium paints a selection foreground (Linux, Windows, ChromeOS, Android) and in their own colour on macOS, an author `::selection` background and colour on every platform, the highlight over the whole line box and the line-break marks of a multi-line selection. Compared with screenshots of the live page on macOS and Linux by `test/unit/snapshot-capture/selection-oracle.driver.mjs`. Windows is not measured; on Firefox and Safari the selection colour is drawn over the glyphs.
- Tests: unit tests in Node and headless Chrome, browser gates with positive controls and a mutation suite, the official WPT HTML-in-Canvas tests at a pinned commit (`docs/CONFORMANCE.md`), Firefox and Safari through WebDriver, and framework apps.

### Known limitations

- The picture lags the DOM by one to two frames, except for the typing and scrolling shortcuts above.
- The children of a drawable canvas are relocated into a live layer next to it; some CSS selectors and live collections can see that layer.
- The core bundle is about 96 KB gzipped, over its original size target.
- Iframes, cross-origin resources without CORS, native UI (select popups, date pickers, IME composition), workers, web views inside apps and Firefox for Android are not supported. Phones and tablets were verified on the iOS Simulator and the Android Emulator only.

See the README and `docs/SUPPORT-MATRIX.md` for the full list.
