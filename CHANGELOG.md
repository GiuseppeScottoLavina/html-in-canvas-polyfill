# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Changed

- liquid-glass: stiffer follow springs, at the owner's request: 600 /s² for a mouse or a pen (was 260) and 1300 /s² for a finger (was 900), with the same damping ratios (0.74 and 0.9), one constant per pointer kind (`SPRINGS`). Along a fast path over the gallery's stage the glass trails a mouse by 55 ms at the median and 62 ms at p95 (was 89 / 104 ms; 120 / 167 px, was 186 / 262) and a finger by 43 / 52 ms (was 53 / 65). The jelly stretch keeps its ratio to the mouse spring, so it wobbles as before; the press swell and the flick are unchanged.
- Scroll compositor: a sticky header without a z-index (or of z-index 0) is composed. Before, a sticky element had to have a positive z-index to be pinned, so a plain `position: sticky; top: 0` header with an opaque background refused the whole plan, and every scroll step of its list was a snapshot. Measured on an iPad Pro (Safari, 120 Hz), a 300-row list with such a header scrolled for 4 s, preview.23 against this change, alternated: 0 composed steps, 76 to 94 canvas updates a second and 16.7 to 16.8 ms between paints at p95, against 464 to 466 composed steps, 116 a second and 9.1 to 9.2 ms (with the field above the list focused: 71 to 83 against 111 a second). A header of z-index `auto` or 0 is pinned only where nothing else of the list paints over it, as CSS paints a later positioned box of z-index `auto` / 0 or a box with a stacking context of its own (`opacity` below 1, a `transform`, a filter, ...) above it: a row of `position: relative` or of opacity 0.9 still refuses the plan. Browser tests compare each composed step with the settled canvas; G-scroll-cadence's covers case has three new modes, and the mutant `scroll-sticky-layer-off` shows that the stacking check is needed.
- Scroll compositor: a refusal for a sticky element is counted as `stats.snapshot.scroll.refusedBy.sticky` (before, as `plan`), and TROUBLESHOOTING section 6 lists what a sticky element needs. The core bundle grows to 357,212 bytes minified, 121,469 gzipped (+881 / +357) and to 17,106 code lines (+26); the module loader's polyfill path with it, to 132,564 bytes gzipped on Chromium and 149,141 on Firefox and Safari (+344).

### Fixed

- Effects: a fast drag of an effect over the drawn HTML no longer rebuilds it at every element the pointer crosses. A `:hover` rule anywhere on the page made every hover change inside a drawn source a rebuild and a new upload, even one that cannot match there: the plugin gallery's only `:hover` rule styles its menu chips, outside the canvas. A `:hover` rule now counts for a drawable only when its selector, with `:hover` made neutral, matches the drawable or an element inside it (an ancestor outside it, as in `body:hover .x`, still counts); a `:hover` the neutral form cannot stand for (`:not(:hover)`, sibling combinators, structural pseudo-classes, `:host`, nesting, `@scope`) counts as before. Measured on an iPad Pro (Safari, 120 Hz) in the plugin gallery, the liquid glass moved for 4 s along a fast path: 96 to 100 frames a second with 88 to 91 frames over 12.5 ms and 83 to 88 uploads, against 119.7 to 119.9 with 0 or 1 such frames and no upload; a pass-through plugin the same. Desktop Chrome: 111 uploads in 4 s against 0, the longest main-thread task 8.9 against 4.4 ms. The selectors are made neutral once per stylesheet change (11 ms for 590 KB of CSS with 10,000 `:hover` selectors) and matched once per drawable and mutation (5.4 ms over 6,000 elements), never per pointer move.
- Effects: a finger moves no `:hover` through an effect, as without one. The interposer made the hover follow a finger across what it passed, also while it dragged a plugin's handle (the liquid glass); now a press activates the element drawn under the finger and hovers nothing, a pan or a drag hovers nothing, a tap leaves the element drawn where it landed hovered until the next tap, and a tap outside the canvas ends it. Mouse and pen are unchanged. G-interposer-strong checks both under the CRT effect (mutants `hover-scope-off`, `finger-hover-follows`).
- The core bundle grows to 360,645 bytes minified, 122,816 gzipped (+3,433 / +1,347) and to 17,248 code lines (+142); the effects bundle to 40,039 / 14,147 (+586 / +161); the module loader's polyfill path with the core, to 133,996 bytes gzipped on Chromium and 150,573 on Firefox and Safari (+1,432).
- Scroll compositor: a flex or grid item with a z-index above a sticky header's (a row of a `display: flex` list with `z-index: 5` and no position) was hidden under the header's tile while the list moved, until the settled tile put it back (11,981 of 104,000 pixels wrong in the test). CSS applies a z-index to a flex or grid item without a position, and the planner now counts it.

## [0.1.0-preview.23] - 2026-11-05

The scroll compositor no longer shows wrong pixels in two cases an outside review found: a sticky header that turned translucent after the list was planned, and content over a list that hit-testing cannot see (`pointer-events: none`, shadows, pseudo-elements, small boxes); it now checks the port in pixels before it composes. On Chromium at 125 % display scaling the last column of a scrolled list is right. The module loader carries 17.9 % less to Chromium on the polyfill path. The effect plugins have new lighting and materials, work with a finger on touch screens, play a short demo and do no work at rest.

- The core bundle grows to 356,331 bytes minified, 121,112 gzipped (+4,135 / +1,497 bytes) and to 17,080 code lines (+132); the effects bundle to 39,453 / 13,986 bytes (+569 / +185) and the classic loader to 17,465 / 6,328 (+172 / +51). The native bridge, the engines and the GPU bundle are unchanged.

### Added

- Effect plugins: an action `demo` on every plugin whose look depends on an input or an event (disintegrate, glitch, holo-card, liquid-glass, page-curl, shatter, water). It is a short showcase, at most about 4 s, that ends at rest, gives way at once to the user's input and resolves at once under reduced motion. The plugin gallery plays it when an effect is selected (`&demo=0` turns that off).
- Effect plugins: touch. A finger claims the gesture only on a plugin's own handle (the liquid glass, a page corner) and scrolls the page everywhere else; whole-surface effects (water, holo-card) never block scrolling. `meta.touch` is the one-line hint a page shows on a touch screen; `meta.continuous` declares what renders at rest by design (CRT, holo-card's idle sway, glitch's ambient events). Both are in `dist/plugins/manifest.json`. EFFECTS-PLUGINS.md section 15.1 states the three rules (touch, `demo`, zero work at rest) for plugin authors, and the template plugin follows them.
- G-gallery (`npm run test:gallery`, in `npm run test:local`): every plugin of the manifest in the gallery at 390 x 844 (a phone, touch emulation) and 1194 x 748 (an iPad in landscape). It checks that the stage is in the first viewport, the form fits without an inner scroll, a touch drag scrolls the page, the demo plays and the effect settles to 0 effect frames, the frame cap follows the display and no demo plays under reduced motion; eight positive controls.

### Changed

- Plugin gallery: the effect comes first. Chips sit above the stage, and the description and actions in a panel beside it on wide screens, below it elsewhere. On an iPad in landscape the stage moved from 588-1210 px down the page to 90-730 px, fully in view. The frame cap defaults to the display rate the page measures (119.9 Hz on an iPad Pro, 8.34 ms frames instead of 16.7 ms), and a readout shows effect frames per second, the frame interval p50 / p95, the cap and the mode.
- Zero work at rest: on an iPad Pro (Safari, 120 Hz) the gallery at rest presented effect frames per second before / after: water 60 / 0, shatter 46 (its intro) / 0, page-curl 19 / 0 (`autoPeek` is now opt-in), holo-card 120 / 30 (the idle sway alone, redrawn at 30 Hz), glitch 120 / 14.5 (ambient events on a timer).
- Liquid glass: a squircle slab of glass with a height field, refracted by Snell's law through its thickness, with per-channel dispersion, Fresnel, key and rim glints, drop, contact and inner shadows, optional `frost` and Catmull-Rom sampling. Its edge is continuous, so the picture under it never folds or doubles. A finger drags it with a press swell, a jelly stretch and inertia; the mouse still moves it as before. iPad at 120 Hz: frame interval p95 8.6 ms while it moves.
- Water: a height-field surface (the wave equation on a grid of about 3 CSS px cells) with refraction from the surface normals, caustic webs, Fresnel reflection and sun glints. Drops ring out as ripple trains and a moving pointer or finger leaves a wake. The input mapper reads the same field as the GPU (0 wrong pixels). A finger that scrolls the page keeps stirring the water (passive touch events, never a blocked scroll). New options `light` and `ambient` (an opt-in continuous swell); default intensity 0.5. iPad: the demo at 120 effect frames/s, p95 8.9 ms.
- Shatter: the falling pane is thick glass. Shards are 5 px slabs with back faces and edge walls (4x MSAA, depth), bevels that catch a studio light by Fresnel, and soft shadows on a lit backdrop; the motion-blur ghost copies are gone, and the shards slow down as they land. One way is 1.2 s of physics in real time (`duration` 1200, was 1600). iPad: 119.5 effect frames/s, p95 8.58 ms.
- Disintegrate: the grains come from a compute pass and ride a 3D curl-noise wind in perspective (size, parallax, defocus); each grain is a bright, antialiased point of its pixel's colour, and the eroding front glows. `play` and `demo` let the dust leave the canvas entirely and hold an empty beat (0.45 s) before the page re-forms (3.85 s in all). New options `depth`, `focus`, `glow`, `glowColor`, `density`. iPad: 120 effect frames/s, p95 8.6 ms; desktop GPU p50 1.51 ms, p95 2.16 ms at 2200 x 1240 (M1 Max). Its GPU test of the page under the dust now compares two runs with opposite winds, which separates a change of the page from dust flying over it (the old measure read 83 % for an unchanged page); a page pass that really darkens the background still fails it.
- Page curl: the roll is lit by its real surface normal (a highlight band, a darker rolled edge and crease), it occludes the page beneath it, the cut edge shows the paper's thickness and the show-through follows the light. A finger on a corner turns the page instead of scrolling it. GPU 1.34 ms per frame at 2200 x 1240 on an M1 Max; iPad: the demo at 119.8 effect frames/s.
- Holo card: a lamp-lit glare (Blinn-Phong on the card's normal, with Schlick Fresnel), a thin-film foil that follows the viewing angle, glinting flakes, a lit bevel, supersampled print. A finger tilts it passively (the page still scrolls), and an opt-in `motion` option tilts it with the device.
- Glitch: at rest a still damaged signal with an event about every 1.4 s (slices, a VHS tracking band, a chroma jolt) that keeps off the focused field and pauses while the user types; corrupted macroblocks, up to 32 blocks.
- Effects host: a finger focuses and activates the control the effect shows on a tap, when it lifts within 10 CSS px of where it landed, as a page without effects does. Before, it focused at the press, so dragging the glass across a field focused that field, made its caret blink and, on a phone, would raise the keyboard. Mouse and pen are unchanged. The effects bundle grows by 569 bytes minified (185 gzipped).
- Build: the WGSL a plugin bundle carries has no comments, indentation or blank lines, which makes every shader plugin 15 to 18 % smaller (shatter 42,212 -> 35,755 bytes minified). The plugins' ceilings are their measured sizes.
- Tests: the gates that used the water as an always-moving effect now freeze a frame and drop a ripple below a field before judging the click (G-f1-form-effect, G-interposer-strong, G-native-interposer; thresholds unchanged), and the frame-cap checks run the water with `ambient`. G-f1-form-effect's water trials scored 16/16 in five consecutive runs.
- Module loader (`<package>/loader`): on the polyfill path it imports a private module with `install()` and the GPU code (`dist/esm/chunks/loader-core-*.js`) instead of the package entry, and the engines (`chunks/engines-*.js`) only on Firefox and Safari. A bundler keeps every export of a module imported with `import()`, so the polyfill path carried the whole `FEATURES` table, the other exports and, on Chromium, the engines. Measured with an app that imports only `load`, bundled and minified by esbuild with code splitting (gzip level 9): the polyfill path on Chromium 161,057 -> 132,220 bytes (-17.9 %), on Firefox and Safari 148,797 (-7.6 %), with the core of this release; the start 7,749 -> 8,029 (+280). The public exports do not change; the `install()` it calls is the package entry's own. `dist/esm/index.js` keeps its exports and most of its code moves to the shared chunks, which `package.json` now lists in `sideEffects`. G-bundle builds that app and holds the three paths to a gzip ceiling, with a control in which the loader imports the package entry. The docs say that the consumer's bundler must keep `import()` split: without splitting the same app is 150,824 bytes gzipped from the start.
- Module loader: `engines: false` now applies (before, it was accepted and the engines were registered anyway): on Firefox and Safari the engines are not imported, as with the classic loader.
- Loader (classic and module): an invalid option, of the loader or of `install()`, rejects that `load()` call with a `TypeError` before anything is decided or downloaded, as `install()` throws it, and the rejected call no longer takes the window's load (a later valid call loads). Before, it resolved `status: 'error'` with `native: null` although there was a window, and every later call got that error. `native` is `null` only without a window, as documented. The classic loader grows to 17,465 bytes minified, 6,328 gzipped (+172 / +51). Tests: browser unit tests for each invalid option, the window's load after a rejected call, the probe in every result with a window, and the engines on Chromium, Firefox and Safari with the real core.

### Fixed

- Scroll compositor: a sticky header that stopped being an opaque rectangle after the list was planned (`opacity: 0.5`, a translucent background, a shadow) was still drawn from its opaque tile over pages that hide it: the first composed step showed the old header (12,000 of 104,000 pixels wrong, up to 128 levels, headless Chrome 156 and Safari 27.2 on an iPad). A pinned element that a change reaches is now checked against the pin requirements before the next composition; one that fails them drops the plan (`stats.snapshot.scroll.stale`), and the planner then refuses it. A header that stays opaque (its text changes) is still composed.
- Scroll compositor: something outside a list that paints over its port without being hit-testable was carried along with the list by every composed step, until the settled tile put it back: a box with `pointer-events: none` (12,000 pixels misplaced in the reported case), a box smaller than the planner's 32 px hit-test grid, the shadow or outline of a box beside the port, a `::before` / `::after` box, a visible child of a hidden box, and a gradient showing through a list without a background (288 to 92,112 of 104,000 pixels wrong per step). Before its first composition the port is now checked in pixels: rendered without the list's content and with its background in a sentinel colour, every port pixel must show that colour (and, for a list that is not opaque, rendered without its content, one colour); otherwise the plan is refused (new reason `stats.snapshot.scroll.refusedBy.cover`). A change inside the port after the plan is checked against the elements outside the list as well as hit-tested; one that may paint there drops the plan (`obstructed`). A hidden, fully transparent or `opacity: 0` box over the list does not stop the composition. The planner's steps are unchanged; the checks cost one more side build per plan (two for a list without an opaque background), read in jobs of about 1 ms, and about 1.2 us per element outside the list at each change inside the port (beyond 1,000 such elements a change there drops the plan instead). Composed and refused counts on the scroll bench cases and on the examples are unchanged. These checks add 4,129 bytes minified (1,482 gzipped) and 108 code lines to the core bundle.
- Scroll compositor on Chromium at a fractional device pixel ratio: a scrollport whose edge falls on a half device pixel is composed to that edge. The live rect reads back in single precision, a hair below the half (a 400.4 css px list column at DPR 1.25 ended at 549.49999 surface px, where the engine has 549.5 and draws to 550), so every page and composed frame was one device column narrower than the settled picture and that column kept older pixels (26-27 pixels per composed frame, up to 21 levels). Edges are now put on the engine's 1/64 px layout grid before they are rounded. Composed frames against the settled tile at the same offset, headless Chrome at DPR 1.25: 0 of 26 pixel-equal before, 26 of 26 after; unchanged at DPR 1, 1.5 and 2. The core bundle grows by 6 bytes minified.
- Page curl: a page that added the plugin with options of its own and without `autoPeek` got a peek of the dog-ear every 5 s, a timer and frames at rest; the plugin's own default was still 5, while `meta.defaults`, the README and this release's opt-in say 0. It is 0 now.
- CRT: `preset` given to `addPlugin` (`{ preset: 'monitor' }`) was ignored and the arcade look shown, because the defaults were spread over the options before they were read and set every value; only `setOptions` applied a preset. It applies at creation now, and an option given next to it still wins.

## [0.1.0-preview.22] - 2026-11-04

On Chrome 157, where the browser implements the API, a page can now download nothing but a 6 KB loader instead of the whole polyfill. On Safari, scrolling a list while a field shows its caret is back to full speed (measured on an iPad: 94 to 118 canvas updates a second). A scroller planned in steps no longer composes a box that changed while it was being planned at its old place.

- The core bundle grows to 352,196 bytes minified, 119,615 gzipped (+942 / +368 bytes) and to 16,948 code lines (+151, of which 118 are the loader's own files); the effects bundle shrinks to 38,884 / 13,801 bytes (-456 / -211) and the native bridge to 13,457 / 4,909 (-399 / -218); the new loader is 17,293 / 6,277 bytes.

### Added

- A loader that downloads nothing where the browser implements the API: `dist/hic-live-loader.iife(.min).js` (`window.HicLiveLoader.load()`) and `dist/esm/loader.js` (`import { load } from '<package>/loader'`, with types). `load(options?)` makes `install()`'s own decision (the same `decideInstall`, with `options.install`) and downloads the core, the engines bundle on Firefox and Safari, and the native bridge for a native build it can bridge, only when the polyfill or the bridge will run; then it calls `install()`. It resolves `{status, mode, installed, native, reason?, error?}` and never rejects: concurrent calls share one download and one install, and a timeout, a load error or a CSP block settle `status: 'error'`; a core that arrives after that never installs. On Chrome 157.0.8103.39 a page with the loader downloads 17,293 bytes (6,277 gzipped) instead of the core's 352,196 (119,615); on the polyfill path the first paint comes one request later (+10.4 ms median from a local server, +45.4 ms with 40 ms of latency per request, headless). The 5 KB gzip goal is not met: about 4,340 bytes of the 6,277 are `install()`'s decision, which the loader shares instead of reimplementing it. Docs: API-CONTRACT section 2.4, BUNDLES, GETTING-STARTED, README, FAQ. Tests: the decision against `install()` on the native-detect matrix, the shared load, the timeout with a late core, a 404 and a CSP (unit and G-bundle), and the native path on Chrome 157 (`test/native-effects/g-native-loader.mjs`).
- Test harness: the static server holds an answer with `?delay=<ms>`.

### Changed

- Scroll compositor on Safari: a focused field whose caret shows no longer slows composed scrolling. Before each composed step the caret's pixels were put back, and after it they were read from the surface again; in Safari that pair stalls the drawing of the step. Now the patch is put back only when the caret lies across the edge of the port being composed: a caret beside the scroller is left as it is, and one inside it is forgotten once the port is composed (the composition writes every pixel of it). Measured on an iPad Pro 11-inch (3rd generation), Safari on iPadOS 27.2, DPR 2, a 760 x 1000 surface, a focused field above a 300-row list, 4 s of scripted scrolling, 5 alternated pairs: 93.5-94.2 canvas updates a second before, 117.6-117.7 after (119.7 with `caret-color: transparent`, 120.0 without focus), rAF p95 16 -> 9 ms. In isolation, a step with the save and the put-back pair ran at 87 frames a second on that iPad and at 120 without it. Tests: a real-install browser test counts the patch's reads and writes over composed steps beside a focused field (8 and 9 in 15 steps before, the blinks only after) and checks that no caret pixel leaves the field. The core bundle grows by 195 bytes minified (84 gzipped) and 7 code lines.
- `src/contracts/events.js` marks its frozen tables pure, so a bundle carries only the tables it reads: the effects bundle is 456 bytes smaller minified (211 gzipped), the native bridge 399 (218). The core grows by 108 bytes minified (15 gzipped) and 122 code lines in `src/` (the loader's files; the core bundle gains only `decideInstall`).

### Fixed

- Scroll compositor: a plan made in steps (since 0.1.0-preview.21) ignored changes made between two of its steps. A box of the list that became `position: fixed` while the hit-test grid was being made was composed at its old place in the first scroll step, until the settled tile put it back (reproduced with the published bundles in Chrome and on an iPad with Safari 27.2). Damage between two steps that an installed plan would not survive now makes the plan again from the start, once (a second such change refuses it); damage inside the port is checked for an obstruction before the first composition; focus or a selection that comes inside the scroller ends the plan. A change of an element's `position` or `z-index` at the same size, and the first change of an element seen absolute, fixed or sticky, now damage the whole drawable: the rect of the old and new box missed that the element no longer scrolls with the list, for an installed plan too. New counter `stats.snapshot.scroll.stale`. The core bundle grows by 639 bytes minified (264 gzipped) and 22 code lines. Tests: a real-install browser test (the box at canvas row 20 instead of 60 in the first composed frame without the change, at 60 with it; a colour change between two steps still composes), compositor unit tests, a planner test for focus and selection between steps, and a damage-rect test.
- Report: a refusal of a scroller inside a shadow root while a field in it had focus was counted as `refusedBy.plan` instead of `refusedBy.focus` (the document's active element is the shadow host).
- Field test and Chrome scroll bench: the counters per phase kept only numbers, so `refusedBy` was dropped from each phase; it is now reported as the refusals of the phase by reason. The field test's summary line now says what `over8_33Share` and `over16_67Share` count: frames over 12.5 and 25 ms (a missed 120 Hz or 60 Hz refresh), not over 8.33 and 16.67 ms.
- `test/mobile/run.mjs --no-control`: the JSON report was written before the run was marked as failed, so it could say `pass: true` while the command failed.
- `scripts/check-bundle.mjs`: the sizes in `dist/sizes.json` are checked against the files in `dist/`, so `--no-build` no longer judges a file rebuilt or edited since by its old size.

## [0.1.0-preview.21] - 2026-11-04

A focused field's caret no longer stays behind while its list scrolls (Safari and Chrome), and costs nothing when it is hidden or transparent. Chrome 157 ships HTML-in-Canvas enabled: the polyfill steps aside there (measured), and its own path is fixed for Chrome 157's text-field metrics. Planning a large scroller no longer blocks the main thread, and the report says why a scroller is not composed.

- The core bundle grows to 351,254 bytes minified, 119,247 gzipped (+2,866 / +1,153 bytes) and to 16,797 code lines (+98); the engines bundle to 45,781 / 17,409 bytes (+266 / +114).

### Added

- Report: the scroll compositor's refusals are counted by reason in `report().canvases[i].stats.snapshot.scroll.refusedBy` (`scale`, `focus`, `selection`, `budget`, `shape`, `pinned`, `plan`), next to the `refusals` total they add up to. Before, a list that was never composed showed only that total. Desktop Safari 27 at DPR 2 with `maxDpr: 1.5`: `refusedBy.scale` 5, 0 composed steps and 234 snapshots in a 4 s scripted scroll; with `maxDpr: 2`, 232 composed steps and 0 snapshots. The core bundle grows by 317 bytes minified (149 gzipped) and 6 code lines.
- Scroll benches (`test/bench/scroll-jank.mjs`, `test/bench/scroll-jank-ios.mjs`): the absolute frame budget next to the gaps relative to the frame interval under load: `movingFrameHz`, the rAF rate at rest `restHz` and `restShare`, and the share of frame intervals over 1.5 x 8.33 / 16.67 ms. Reported data, no thresholds.

### Changed

- Tests: Chrome 157 ships the native API enabled, and the CI runners moved to it on 2026-11-04. The test harness now launches Chrome with `CanvasDrawElement` in `--disable-features` unless a run asks for the native implementation, so the tests, gates and mutants run the polyfill's own path (`--disable-blink-features` is not used: its warning bar made the first tab 56 css px shorter than the others), and passes `--disable-infobars`.
- Docs: Chrome 157 ships HTML-in-Canvas enabled by default, so the native implementation is no longer described as Canary only. Measured on 2026-11-04 on Chrome for Testing 157.0.8103.39 without any flag: the API is exposed (`drawElementImage`, `requestPaint`, `getElementTransform`, `onpaint`, `content`), `install()` decides kind `complete`, probe `ok`, no missing names and no gaps, and steps aside (mode `native`); the native bridge is not needed there. `npm run test:native-effects` passed on it as on Canary 159.0.8137.0 (G-native-effects 14 of 14, G-native-interposer 10 of 10 with their controls red, the water and disintegrate gallery e2e, `examples/effect-plugin`). The polyfill's own path is what Safari, Firefox and Chrome before 157 use. Updated: the README (introduction, support table, comparison table), SUPPORT-MATRIX (the Chrome 157 row is measured instead of expected), GETTING-STARTED, FAQ, BUNDLES, API-CONTRACT, DESIGN-DECISIONS, ARCHITECTURE, TEST-PLAN, EFFECTS-PLUGINS, CONTRIBUTING and the feature summaries.
- Docs: native Chrome fires no `paint` event for a write of `canvas.width` or `canvas.height` made outside a `paint` handler, where the polyfill fires one (measured on Chrome 157.0.8103.39 and Canary 159.0.8137.0, from a timer and from a ResizeObserver callback after the first `paint`). The explainer's basic example is not affected, because its ResizeObserver callback runs before the first `paint`; API-CONTRACT and GETTING-STARTED say to call `canvas.requestPaint()` after a later size write.
- `npm run test:native-effects -- --chrome=<path>` runs the suite on another Chrome with the native API, such as Chrome 157 (the gates, the gallery e2e and the example); without it the suite still uses Canary.
- Docs: in Safari and Firefox inner scrolling is composed only at a whole-number raster scale. API-CONTRACT section 8 said composed frames can differ from the settled picture by a few device pixels whenever the raster scale is below the device pixel ratio; that holds for Chromium only. PERFORMANCE, MOBILE and TROUBLESHOOTING now state the trade-off of a fractional `maxDpr` or a budget-reduced scale on WebKit, with the measured numbers. A budget-reduced scale is not rounded down to a whole number: with the default budgets a surface is reduced only above 33,554,432 px (16,777,216 on phones and tablets), and an explicit `maxDpr` stays the value the page asked for.
- Scroll compositor: planning a scroller no longer blocks the main thread. The plan ends with a grid of up to 32 x 32 hit tests over the scroller's port, and a hit test costs in proportion to the scroller's children, so the whole grid ran in one task when the pointer entered a long list. Measured with an 818 css px port at DPR 2, one task before: headless Chrome 156, 113-120 ms over 10,000 rows (730-761 ms at 6x CPU throttling), 40-46 ms over 2,000 rows of 3 elements (175 ms), 17-20 ms over 300 rows (60-62 ms); Safari 27.0.1 (macOS), 187-202 ms, 46-56 ms and 16 ms. The grid now yields every 3 ms and the scan of the subtree as well (at most 2,500 elements a step, as before), each step a post-frame task: the longest step after is 3.3 ms in Chrome (7.7 ms at 6x) and 3.1-5.2 ms in Safari (once 11.3 ms, in 12 plans). The plan is ready later: after 384-473 ms instead of about 120 ms over 10,000 rows (2.3-3.0 s at 6x), 47-62 ms instead of 17-20 ms over 300 rows (Safari: 610-678 ms instead of 205-319 ms, 39-41 ms instead of 16 ms); a gesture that starts before is built as an unplanned scroller's is. A usable native implementation is never refused, whatever secondary members it lacks, renames or adds; `installed.report()` records what differs (`native-step-aside`). The optional `hic-live-native-bridge` adds what it can build over the earlier `layoutsubtree` draft. Only a native implementation without its core is refused (`HicUnsupportedError('partial-native')`).
- Spec alignment notes: `ElementImage` has `close()` only, its size is `canvas.getElementImageDefaultSize(elementImage)` (whatwg/html#11588, 2026-10-08), with no `width` / `height` alias because native Chrome has none. The two-argument `getElementTransform(element, drawTransform)` and `OffscreenCanvas.getElementTransform` remain as deprecated legacy overloads until the pinned WPT revision moves. `options.mode: 'polyfill'` over a usable native implementation steps aside with one warning.
- Desktop Chrome, Firefox 155+ and Safari 27+. On Firefox and Safari the opt-in `hic-live-engines` bundle draws form controls, classic scrollbars and shadows in each engine's look.
- Typed text in single-line fields is drawn at once, inner scrolling is composed from cached pages, and snapshot builds run after the frame, so typing and wheel scrolling are close to native latency (`docs/PERFORMANCE-COMPARISON.md`).
- Extensions of this library, not part of the standard and not provided by native implementations: a frame cap (`framecap` attribute, `canvas.frameCap`) and a WebGPU effect-plugin host with input remapping (`HTMLInCanvasEffects`, plugin ABI v2). They work only while the polyfill provides the API (mode `polyfill`).
- Eight example effect plugins outside the polyfill, in `plugins/` (one folder each, found by the build by convention): `crt`, `disintegrate`, `glitch`, `holo-card`, `liquid-glass`, `page-curl`, `shatter` and `water`. Bundles `dist/plugins/<name>.iife(.min).js` and `.esm.js`, package subpaths `./plugins/<name>`, a manifest (with each plugin's reduced-motion behaviour), a plugin template, `plugins/_lib/` for runtime code that several plugins share, and a test kit that checks a plugin's input mapper against the GPU pixels and drives it in the gallery with trusted input (`npm run test:plugins`).
- Plugin actions, a uniform way to run what a plugin can do: a definition may declare `actions` (ABI v2), and the effect handle of `host.addPlugin` gains `actions`, `trigger(action, options?)` and `settled()`. `trigger` resolves with the state the plugin rests in once the frame that shows it was rendered, or with `null` when a newer action superseded it, the plugin was removed, disabled or failed, or the GPU device was lost; an unknown action, a plugin without actions or bad options reject with a `TypeError` that names what is accepted. The host reads `prefers-reduced-motion` at each trigger and passes it to the plugin; the plugin's own submit handlers and buttons take the same path. Actions of the example plugins: disintegrate `out` / `in` / `play`, shatter `impact` / `crack` / `out` / `in` / `play` (with an optional impact `point`), glitch `burst`, page-curl `peek`, water `drop`; the template shows one (`shift`). The gallery shows a button per action. Reference: `docs/guides/EFFECTS-PLUGINS.md` section 5, `docs/API-CONTRACT.md` 7.3.1.
- `examples/effect-plugin`: the smallest page with effects (about 30 lines of script): the host, the water plugin, the frame cap and a disintegrate transition run with `handle.trigger` from the page's submit handler, with the 2D fallback.
- `NO_TARGET` in the effects ABI (`HTMLInCanvasEffects.NO_TARGET`, `isNoTarget`; exported by the effects module): what an input mapper returns where the presented pixel shows no element. The mapper chain stops at it, so an earlier plugin cannot clamp it back onto the canvas, and the interposer swallows that gesture (no focus change, no activation, no click).
- `npm run test:pages`: the GitHub Pages site under its project path in headless Chrome, every example and the gallery with each plugin.
- Classic `<script>` bundles (readable and minified) and an ES module build with `exports` and TypeScript declarations; no runtime dependencies and no network request on load.
- Six examples, among them a gallery of the effect plugins over one live form and the smallest page with effects, and a zero-dependency demo server (`npm run demo`); guides for getting started, frameworks (React, Vue, Svelte, Lit), effect plugins (using and writing them), performance and troubleshooting.
- Phones and tablets, verified on the iOS Simulator and the Android Emulator only (Android: Chromium 158, whose native HTML-in-Canvas makes the polyfill step aside; its own path was run forced): Chrome for Android 130+ and Safari on iOS / iPadOS 26+ are admitted (iPadOS's desktop user agent is recognised by its touch points; Chrome, Firefox and Edge on iOS are WebKit and are admitted by the required features). Android WebView, in-app iOS web views and Firefox for Android stay refused, with the reason named. Phones get their own surface budgets (`maxDpr` 3, 4096 x 4096 px per surface, 256 MiB in all, 128 MiB on devices reporting 2 GB or less), and iOS form controls are drawn in the iOS look (system blue tint, pill buttons and pop-up buttons, the colour-well hue ring, medium-style date and time pills, no number spinner). Guide: `docs/guides/MOBILE.md`.
- `npm run test:mobile`: Mobile Safari in the iOS Simulator (`safaridriver`) and Chrome in the Android Emulator (adb and the DevTools protocol): taps on moved drawables, typing, touch scroll, the virtual keyboard, rotation, long-press selection, the iOS control look (62 regions) and the refusal of older browsers, each with a positive control.
- `report().canvases[].stats.snapshot.rasterScale`: the raster scale of the committed snapshots.
- Safari Technology Preview as an optional engine of `npm run test:xbrowser` (`stp`): skipped with the reason when it is not installed or its remote automation is off; its native surface is read first. With its HTML-in-Canvas internal feature off it runs the polyfill scenarios and reports the native-flag scenarios skipped; with it on it runs them: install steps aside, the explainer and the contact form drawn natively through the bridge (pixels against the plain-DOM twin, trusted clicks, typing and repaint), uninstall identity and the examples, each with a positive control.
- Text selections are drawn in the colours the engine paints them, in single-line fields and in page text (labels, paragraphs, contenteditable): the system Highlight under the glyphs, the glyphs in HighlightText where Chromium paints a selection foreground (Linux, Windows, ChromeOS, Android) and in their own colour on macOS, an author `::selection` background and colour on every platform, the highlight over the whole line box and the line-break marks of a multi-line selection. Compared with screenshots of the live page on macOS and Linux by `test/unit/snapshot-capture/selection-oracle.driver.mjs`. Windows is not measured; on Firefox and Safari the selection colour is drawn over the glyphs.
- Tests: unit tests in Node and headless Chrome, browser gates with positive controls and a mutation suite, the official WPT HTML-in-Canvas tests at a pinned commit (`docs/CONFORMANCE.md`), Firefox and Safari through WebDriver, and framework apps.

### Known limitations

- The picture lags the DOM by one to two frames, except for the typing and scrolling shortcuts above.
- The children of a drawable canvas are relocated into a live layer next to it; some CSS selectors and live collections can see that layer.
- The core bundle is about 96 KB gzipped, over its original size target.
- Iframes, cross-origin resources without CORS, native UI (select popups, date pickers, IME composition), workers, web views inside apps and Firefox for Android are not supported. Phones and tablets were verified on the iOS Simulator and the Android Emulator only.

See the README and `docs/SUPPORT-MATRIX.md` for the full list.
